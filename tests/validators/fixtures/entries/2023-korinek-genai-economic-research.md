# Generative AI for Economic Research: Use Cases and Implications for Economists

## Summary

Korinek systematically maps how large language models can assist economists, describing dozens of use cases across six areas: ideation and feedback, writing, background research, data analysis, coding, and mathematical derivations. The paper argues economists can realize significant productivity gains by automating research micro-tasks, and that these gains will grow as model capability improves. It also introduces a capability-rating scheme for individual tasks (from experimental to highly useful) intended to be updated as models advance.

## Why it matters

The most-cited framework for structuring *where* generative AI concretely helps a working economist — useful both as a checklist for adopting AI in research workflows and as a baseline against which newer agentic approaches can be compared. For research teams, the task-level (rather than job-level) decomposition is the practical adoption playbook.

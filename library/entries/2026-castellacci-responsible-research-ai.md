# Generative Artificial Intelligence in Scientific Research: Individual Benefits, Collective Risks, and a Framework for Responsible Research with AI

## Summary

Examines the gap between individual and collective effects of generative AI
adoption in scientific research. The authors report that AI-assisted work
shows measurable gains in publication volume and citation share, while
evidence on novelty, disruption, and breakthrough output "remains ambiguous
or negative" (unverified — abstract-level claim, underlying studies not
independently checked). They identify three mechanisms separating private
gains from collective risk: information asymmetry, negative externalities
affecting shared knowledge, and depletion of research capacity. To address
these, the paper proposes "Responsible Research with AI" (RRAI), extending
existing responsible-innovation frameworks around four principles:
disclosure, differentiation, narrative, and proportionality, built on
institutional structures including the EU AI Act and OECD guidance.

## Why it matters

For the social-science audience: a named, structured account of *why*
individually rational AI use in research can still degrade the collective
knowledge system — useful for anyone assessing AI-assisted work in review,
funding, or replication contexts, not just a general caution. For the
AI-engineering audience: a four-principle governance vocabulary
(disclosure, differentiation, narrative, proportionality) that institutions
building or adopting AI-assisted research tooling can use to scope
disclosure and audit requirements, anchored to existing regulatory
instruments rather than invented from scratch.

# Implications of AI innovation on economic growth: a panel data study

## Summary

Panel study (1970–2019) using AI patent counts as the measure of AI development across countries, estimating growth effects with fixed-effects and GMM specifications. Reported findings: a positive association between AI patenting and growth, larger than the effect of total patents; effects concentrate in advanced economies and strengthen in later periods; the paper reports AI patents contributing roughly 0.1–0.3 percentage points to annual growth (as stated by the source — an association under the paper's specifications, not a causal effect). The author reads the results as consistent with endogenous growth theory, with infrastructure and institutional capacity explaining the concentration of benefits in developed economies.

## Why it matters

*(Radar's assessment.)* Historical context rather than current evidence: the patent-based, 1970–2019 window speaks to pre-LLM AI, not the post-2022 generative-AI expansion. Its lasting value is methodological — what AI-patent proxies can and cannot show, and why different generations of AI should not be treated as one homogeneous technology when estimating growth effects.

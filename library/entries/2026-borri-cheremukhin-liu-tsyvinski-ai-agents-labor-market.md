# The Rise of Agents and the Human Labor Market

## Summary

This paper (Nicola Borri, Anton Cheremukhin, Yukun Liu, Aleh Tsyvinski)
studies how agentic AI — AI systems that carry out multi-step tasks — affects
demand for human workers. The authors combine usage data from OpenRouter
(about two quadrillion AI tokens across more than 400 models) with 34.8
million U.S. job postings from public firms, describing the result as the
first economy-wide, cross-AI-model evidence on agentic AI and labor demand.
They find firms more exposed to agentic AI show slower job-posting growth: a
one-standard-deviation increase in exposure predicts 2.11% lower posting
growth over six months and 3.20% over nine months. An event study around the
release of Claude Code 2.0 shows firms with higher pre-release exposure saw
4.73% lower posting growth over the following six months. The decline is
concentrated in non-senior roles and is not statistically significant for
senior roles; broader (non-agentic) AI usage shows no significant link to
posting growth once agentic usage is controlled for. The authors report that
their market-based exposure measure matches independent evidence from how
firms discuss AI deployment in securities filings and earnings calls.

## Why it matters

A quantified, dated labor-demand estimate that specifically isolates agentic
AI (as opposed to AI use in general) as the channel associated with slower
hiring, concentrated in non-senior roles — directly citable evidence for
anyone modelling or arguing about near-term AI-driven labor-market effects,
and a natural complement to the asset-pricing angle in
[2026-borri-tsyvinski-liu-ai-premium](2026-borri-tsyvinski-liu-ai-premium.md)
from an overlapping author team.

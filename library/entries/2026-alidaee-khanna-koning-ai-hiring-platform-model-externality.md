# Learning about Markets from Models: Evidence from an AI Hiring Platform

## Summary

Studies an AI hiring platform serving large employers of low-skill
workers, which uses a standardized psychometric assessment to build
firm-specific models predicting each applicant's retention at that firm.
Each firm's model predicts retention well for that firm but performs no
better than chance at other firms. By scoring every application against
every firm's model, the authors construct a firm-application matrix and
show that the gains from reassigning an application to a different firm
vary widely by applicant traits, arising from two distinct mechanisms;
firms also disagree about whether some traits raise or lower retention at
the margin. The authors argue this "model comparison externality" — that a
platform gains insight even when pooling data across firms would not
improve any single model's accuracy — requires access to both the full
applicant pool and each firm's model, and has implications for targeting
policy interventions and for how firms see their own divergence from
competitors (as stated in the fetched abstract; full paper and data not
reviewed at capture).

## Why it matters

Gives researchers studying algorithmic hiring and labor-market platforms a
concrete empirical case, and a transferable concept (model comparison
externality), for arguing that comparing firm-specific predictive models
reveals information pooling data cannot — relevant to anyone evaluating
platform-level data access, labor-market matching efficiency, or
competition questions around proprietary hiring algorithms.

# Beyond Headcount and Human Capital: The Effective Cognitive Population as a Decomposable Capacity Unit for AI-Era Planning

## Summary
The paper introduces the Effective Cognitive Population (ECP), a metric that weights population by capability and by the conditions under which capability is deployed, formalized as ECP = N·H·(1 + A·C) — combining a country's population (N), the World Bank's Human Capital Index Plus (H), and components of the IMF's AI Preparedness Index (A, C) across 144 countries. Against headcount/human-capital baselines, the ECP model raises predictive R² from 0.849 to 0.882, and cuts leave-one-country-out RMSE from 0.723 to 0.641. 89 of 144 countries shift at least ten rank positions relative to simple headcount rankings. The A×C interaction term is not statistically significant, which the author reads as evidence the conjunction functions as a planning rule rather than a causal-complementarity claim. Results are reported as stable across alternative denominators, data vintages, and a 27-specification multiverse analysis.

## Why it matters
Gives growth/development researchers and planners a decomposable, reproducible capacity metric for AI-era cross-country comparison — with the caveat, stated by the author, that the rank shifts (not the raw metric) are the primary claimed contribution, and that the framework is a planning heuristic rather than a causal model of AI-driven output.

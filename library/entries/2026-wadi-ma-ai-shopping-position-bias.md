# Does Rank Still Matter? Position Bias When AI Agents Shop on Our Behalf

## Summary

Tests whether AI shopping agents respond to search-result position the way
human shoppers do. The authors randomized the position of 100 hotel
listings across 5,000 AI agent sessions spanning four large language
models. They find "position still predicts which listings are inspected,
but weakly and non-monotonically" — middling positions, not the bottom of
the list, perform worst — and that "the attributes displayed on a results
page matter more than placement within it" for the agents' purchasing
decisions.

## Why it matters

As AI agents take over more consumer shopping decisions, platforms and
regulators concerned with ranking manipulation, dark patterns, or steering
need to know whether the mechanisms that governed human attention (e.g.,
top-of-list advantage) still apply. This experiment's answer — that
displayed attributes dominate raw position, and that position effects are
weak and non-monotonic — is a concrete, quantified starting point for that
question, applicable to market/platform design analysis even though it
covers only one product category and four models.

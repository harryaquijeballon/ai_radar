# The Specification Paradox: Rethinking Requirements Engineering in the Age of AI

## Summary

The paper argues that LLM-driven code generation does not eliminate
software-engineering complexity but shifts it toward "domain understanding,
requirements elicitation, specification development, validation,
maintenance, and software evolution." Its central claim: "the greater the
[code-generation] capability, the greater the dependence on correct,
complete, verifiable, and explainable human-produced specifications." The
authors name emerging risks — automation bias, ambiguity propagation,
Specification Overfitting, and Specification Debt — and argue future
software-engineering success depends as much on humans correctly
specifying, evaluating, and evolving requirements as on models generating
code (unverified — this is a position/argument paper; no benchmark or
empirical result to independently check).

## Why it matters

Gives builders of AI-assisted development workflows named vocabulary for
failure modes that are easy to observe but hard to name precisely —
useful for framing internal reviews of where a coding-agent workflow is
actually fragile (the specification, not the generation step) and for
scoping where human review effort should concentrate.

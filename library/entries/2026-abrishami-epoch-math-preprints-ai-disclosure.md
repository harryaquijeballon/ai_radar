# In August, 25% of Math Preprints Acknowledged AI Use, Up From 4% in April

## Summary

Epoch AI analyzed every mathematics preprint posted to arXiv from January
2025 through August 2026 (roughly 3,000-5,700 preprints per month), filtering
for AI-related keywords and then classifying acknowledgments using GPT-5.6
Sol with Claude Fable 5 validating the classifications; a human review of
200 random classifications found 93% agreement with the model classifier.
The share of preprints acknowledging any AI use rose from 4% in April 2026
to 25% in August 2026, with growth accelerating from May 2026 onward. Within
that, the share of preprints crediting AI with a "substantial research
contribution" (requiring explicit descriptors such as "essential" or
"crucial," not just proofreading or formatting help) rose from 0.77% in
April to 6% in August. The authors flag several limitations: the analysis
measures voluntary disclosure, not underlying AI use, so it conflates actual
usage growth with growing willingness to disclose; and the boundary between
"substantial research contribution" and lighter research assistance is
described as fuzzy and hard to classify consistently.

## Why it matters

For anyone setting or evaluating AI-use disclosure norms in research
institutions or journals, this is a rare quantified, methodologically
transparent trend line (not a single survey wave) showing both the pace of
change and the gap between "any acknowledged use" and "substantial
contribution" claims — useful context for calibrating how much weight
disclosure statements alone can currently bear, and a concrete baseline
against which to compare disclosure norms in other research fields,
including economics and social science.

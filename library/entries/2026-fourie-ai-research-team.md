# AI and the Research Team

## Summary
Fourie models how AI affects research-team composition using a span-of-control framework, arguing that AI lowers execution cost, which expands laboratory scale, and automates codifiable tasks, which lowers the member share of each unit. Team size follows a quasi-concave relationship with AI capability that peaks at most once; for fully codifiable fields the model's 90% forecast intervals place that peak between 2026 and 2030. In mathematics — described as a highly codifiable field — individual researchers using AI are shown achieving research-quality outputs without a team. Fields with less inherently human work content are predicted to reach peak team sizes earlier than fields with more.

## Why it matters
A concrete, falsifiable model — not just a directional claim — for how AI capability should reshape the economics of research production, useful for anyone forecasting how AI changes lab/team structure and funding models in science, including the pace at which some fields might shift toward smaller, AI-augmented teams.

## Verification notes
Read via the arXiv abstract page; quoted mechanism and forecast-interval claims traced to the abstract text. Not independently corroborated. The mathematics single-researcher example is reported as the paper's own illustrative case, not verified against external evidence of specific individuals' output.

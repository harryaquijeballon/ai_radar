# How exposed is work to robotics? Occupational, industry, and geographic measures

## Summary

The paper constructs the first abilities-based measures of robotics exposure
across occupations, industries, and geographies. It follows the Felten,
Raj and Seamans framework: a 16-capability robotics taxonomy spanning eight
domains, with an LLM scoring each capability against 52 O*NET work
abilities. From this the authors build three indices: occupational robotics
exposure (ROE) for 774 occupations, industry robotics exposure (RIE) for 250
NAICS industries, and county-level geographic robotics exposure (RGE).

Headline findings (unverified beyond the abstract): robotics exposure
concentrates in physically intensive work — construction, farming,
transportation, production — with logging, mining, trucking, and warehousing
among the most exposed industries. Unlike generative-AI exposure, robotics
exposure is higher in lower-wage, lower-education occupations with less
creative-ability content. The capabilities that shift rankings most are
physical collaboration/assistance, terrain-based locomotion, grasping and
handling, and task/functional reasoning, though no single capability
dominates the index. The authors are explicit that the measures capture
technical relatedness, not readiness, cost, or adoption — an upper bound on
potential physical automation, not a prediction of it.

## Why it matters

Gives researchers a citable, methodologically transparent exposure measure
to pair against existing generative-AI exposure indices (e.g. Felten/Eloundou
style measures already used elsewhere in this library) when arguing which
kinds of work are vulnerable to which kind of automation — useful for growth,
labour-market, and development analysis that needs to distinguish
"AI-exposed" from "robotics-exposed" occupations rather than treating
automation as a single undifferentiated threat.

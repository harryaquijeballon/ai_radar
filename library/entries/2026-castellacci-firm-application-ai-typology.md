# From the Firm- to the Application-Level: A New Typology of AI Use in Organisations

## Summary
Argues that firm-level studies of AI adoption are too coarse because a
single firm typically runs many distinct AI applications at once, each with
its own novelty, sourcing mode, objective, and technology type. The paper
proposes shifting the unit of analysis from the firm to the individual
application and builds a typology around those four dimensions, placing any
application in a common conceptual space. It highlights two categories the
conventional adoption-versus-innovation split overlooks: co-invention (new
uses of technology the firm did not itself develop) and internalisation
(building AI capability in-house when buying it was an option). The
framework is illustrated with documented use cases; the paper is a
conceptual contribution with no described dataset or statistical method
(unverified beyond the abstract — full text not read at capture).

## Why it matters
Gives researchers designing firm-level AI-adoption surveys or diffusion
studies a concrete, citable unit of analysis (the application, not the
firm) and two named categories — co-invention and internalisation — that
existing adoption/innovation frameworks miss, directly useful for anyone
structuring new firm AI-use data collection or re-reading existing adoption
surveys for these overlooked categories.

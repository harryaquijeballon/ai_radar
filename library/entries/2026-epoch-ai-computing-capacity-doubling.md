# New global AI computing capacity is doubling every 9 months

## Summary

Epoch AI estimates that quarterly additions of global AI chip computing
capacity have grown about 2.5x per year since 2024 (90% CI: 2.3x–2.7x),
implying a doubling time of 9.1 months (90% CI: 8.4–10.0 months) — down from
roughly 6x annual growth in 2023. Quarterly additions rose from 0.8 million
H100-equivalents (H100e) in Q1 2024 to 6.3 million in Q2 2026. The estimate
uses quarterly AI chip sales data for six designers (Nvidia, Google, AMD,
Amazon, Huawei, Cambricon) from Epoch's AI chip sales hub (company financial
disclosures, analyst estimates, media reports), fitting a log-linear
regression to ten quarters (Q1 2024–Q2 2026) of quarterly (not cumulative)
additions, to avoid overstating the current pace after the 2023 surge. The
authors note year-over-year growth in quarterly additions fell from 2.8x
(Q2 2025) to 2.2x (Q2 2026) but say it is unclear whether growth is actually
slowing (the decline is not statistically significant, p=0.26). Sales are
tracked at the point of sale, not deployment, which can lag by months;
smaller designers and non-datacenter GPUs are excluded.

## Why it matters

Gives researchers a specific, dated, confidence-bounded compute-growth rate
to use as an input to AI-and-growth modelling or infrastructure-investment
analysis, rather than a vaguer "AI compute is growing fast" claim — along
with explicit caveats about measurement lag and trend significance that
guard against over-reading it.

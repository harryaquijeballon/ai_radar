# Most AI use at work happens on free plans, except in science and tech

## Summary

An Epoch AI/Ipsos survey of 1,103 employed US adults (fielded 10-19 July
2026; 469 respondents reported using AI for work in the preceding seven
days and formed the analysis base) finds that across most occupation
groups, free AI plans are the dominant payment method used at work
(45-61% of workers), rather than employer-provided subscriptions. Computer,
engineering, and science professionals are the exception: a majority (67%)
use an employer-provided subscription at least some of the time, compared
with 41% in comparable management/business/arts occupations. Results are
segmented into six occupation categories (n=49 to n=207 per group, plus
n=462 overall), using probability-based sampling weighted for
underrepresented demographics. The authors flag wide confidence intervals
given group sizes, potential measurement error from self-reported/write-in
occupation coding, and that the survey captures only a worker's primary
subscription type.

## Why it matters

Directly bears on who is capturing AI's workplace productivity gains and
how: if most workers outside technical fields are using free-tier AI
rather than employer-funded tools, that has implications for how firms
should be interpreting productivity effects, for whether employer AI
investment is actually reaching most job categories, and for AI-adoption
policy debates that assume employer provisioning is the norm.

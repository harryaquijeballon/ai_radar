# CFOs Meet LLMs

## Summary

NBER Working Paper 36057 (John R. Graham, Campbell R. Harvey, Manish Jha)
addresses the slow, costly nature of survey-based business-sentiment
measurement. The authors prompt an LLM to role-play as the CFO of each
public company whose CFO responded to the Duke–Federal Reserve CFO Survey
from 2002 to 2025, conditioning each simulated response on the actual survey
date. The LLM-generated forecasts reasonably match the cross-section of
actual CFO expectations, and accuracy improves as more information is
provided to the model. The paper also discusses the potential and obstacles
of using such "digital twins" to mimic individual decision-makers more
generally.

## Why it matters

A directly evaluable test of LLM-simulated economic agents against two
decades of real survey ground truth — useful both to economists weighing
LLM-based survey substitutes/supplements (lens 6) and to AI builders
designing validation protocols for persona-conditioned simulation agents
(lens 8): it shows concretely how simulation fidelity scales with the
context given to the model, and where it still falls short of the real
cross-section.

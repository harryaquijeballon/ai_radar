# Antalya Artificial Intelligence Pledge: aligning AI development with climate action

## Summary

The ITU and the COP31 Presidency launched the "Antalya Artificial Intelligence
Pledge" on 16 November 2026 in Antalya, Türkiye, aiming to align AI
development with climate action. An initial coalition of governments,
companies, research institutions, and civil society organizations signed on.
ITU Secretary-General Doreen Bogdan-Martin and COP31 President Murat Kurum
are quoted; Bogdan-Martin frames AI as "a powerful tool for tackling the
climate crisis" that must be used responsibly.

Signatories commit to measurable actions against internationally recognized
AI standards, covering four pillars: (1) powering and building AI
infrastructure and hardware sustainably, including data-centre energy and
water efficiency; (2) designing, managing, and procuring AI responsibly
across its lifecycle, including e-waste and hardware reuse; (3) assessing and
reporting AI's environmental impacts using standardized methods; (4)
strengthening financial and technical support for AI-for-climate action,
especially in developing countries, LDCs, and small island developing
states. ITU says its own standards, including new guidelines for measuring
AI's environmental footprint, will support implementation. Progress is to be
reviewed annually at UNFCCC COP meetings through 2030, with further uptake
channelled through Green Digital Action after COP31.

## Why it matters

For economists and policy analysts tracking AI capital expenditure and
infrastructure build-out, this is the first dated marker of an
intergovernmental standards track for measuring and reporting AI's
environmental footprint — a cost category so far mostly absent from AI
investment/productivity accounting. The four-pillar structure and the
2030 review cadence give a concrete hook for watching whether reporting
standards (pillar 3) actually materialize and whether they get adopted by
major AI infrastructure investors, which would make AI capex comparable
across firms/countries on an environmental basis for the first time.

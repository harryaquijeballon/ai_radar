# TRACE: Governing AI-Assisted Research with Principles from Computer Science

## Summary

The paper proposes TRACE, a governance framework for keeping human
researchers in control of AI-assisted work, built on five principles drawn
from software-engineering and security practice: tracking records, reviewing
outputs against sources, accepting results independently, controlling
transitions between stages, and evaluating corrections. The stated aim is to
bound units of AI-assisted work and monitor how each is shaped, so the pace
of delegated work stays matched to human attention and review capacity. The
authors also introduce TRACE-OS, an open-source system implementing these
principles through shared instructions, tooling, and records intended to
fit into existing research workflows, with the argument that a shared
standard like this builds the trust needed to delegate work to AI and
realize productivity gains from it.

## Why it matters

Gives both audiences a named, citable vocabulary (the five TRACE principles)
for discussing what "keeping a human in control of AI-assisted research"
concretely requires, and a specific open-source artifact (TRACE-OS) to
examine or adopt. Useful as a reference point for process design even though
— per the abstract — the framework's real-world effectiveness has not yet
been evaluated empirically; treat it as a candidate practice, not validated
evidence.

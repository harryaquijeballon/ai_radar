# How AI Impacts the Quest for Knowledge: Evidence from AlphaFold2

## Summary

NBER Working Paper 36045 (Myra Mohnen, Joshua S. Gans) examines how
scientists respond to an AI tool by choosing between extending the frontier
of knowledge and deepening understanding of questions already studied. The
authors study this in structural biology using AlphaFold2 (released 2021),
which predicts the 3D structure of nearly every known protein with varying
confidence. Linking 530,471 proteins to experiments run on them from 2017 to
2025, they find that where AlphaFold2's predictions are more confident,
experiments produce fewer first-ever structures for proteins with no known
relatives, while follow-up experiments increase on proteins whose structures
are already known — most for proteins surrounded by relatives of known
structure. The authors argue a confident prediction can substitute for an
experiment that would otherwise establish a new structure, while lowering
the cost of experiments that build on existing structures — so AlphaFold2
deepens existing knowledge rather than extending it.

## Why it matters

A large-sample, dated empirical result on a question central to AI-and-
innovation policy: does an AI capability expand or narrow the frontier of
scientific knowledge? The confidence-dependent substitution/complementarity
pattern it documents is a usable empirical benchmark for anyone modelling
AI's effect on the direction (not just the rate) of innovation.

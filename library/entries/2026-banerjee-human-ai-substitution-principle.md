# The Human-AI Substitution Principle: When will you be replaced by AI in your organization?

## Summary

Presents a "Human-AI Task Allocation" (HAT) model for hierarchical
organizations that formalizes an economic asymmetry: humans acquire skills
gradually while AI capabilities scale differently, and this asymmetry
determines when AI substitutes for human labor. The paper's central
contribution, the "Human-AI Substitution Principle," gives precise
conditions for replacement based on risk-adjusted costs, skills,
organizational depth, deployment scale, strategic adaptation, and risk.
Predicted organizational outcomes include abrupt (not gradual) workforce
transitions in some conditions, hybrid human-AI arrangements in others,
flatter hierarchies with wider management spans of control, particular
vulnerability of middle-management roles, and vulnerability of
highly-skilled workers that depends on organizational depth, baseline
costs, and risk differentials rather than skill level alone. The authors
frame the model as unifying automation economics, organizational design, AI
governance, and workforce planning.

## Why it matters

A citable, applicable analytical framework for the competition/regulation
and growth-and-productivity lenses: rather than another qualitative
displacement narrative, it gives conditions a labor economist or workforce
planner could operationalize — e.g., testing which roles in a given
hierarchy meet the model's substitution conditions, or using the
flatter-hierarchy and middle-management predictions as testable hypotheses
against real restructuring data.

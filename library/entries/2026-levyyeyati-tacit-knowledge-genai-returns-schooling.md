# Practice Makes Perfect: Tacit Knowledge, Generative AI, and Returns to Schooling and Experience

## Summary

Models how generative AI changes which kinds of human capital are scarce:
codified knowledge becomes cheap to access at the point of work, while
judgment built through practice stays tied to the individual worker. In a
short-run model, machine-supplied codified knowledge substitutes for the
codified part of schooling (weakening the schooling return), while the
experience return should rise where codified and practice-based knowledge
are complements and learning-by-doing is fastest; technology-specific
experience can also lose value over time.

Using US Current Population Survey earnings data from 2018 through June
2026, the authors compare occupations in the top and bottom quartiles of
the pre-ChatGPT Felten-Raj-Seamans AI-exposure index. After ChatGPT's
public release, the return to a year of schooling falls by 0.55 log points
in exposed occupations. The return to experience rises by 0.26 log points
at three years, is unchanged at fifteen years, and falls by 0.12 log
points at twenty-five years; under the baseline quadratic specification
the effect crosses zero at 18.1 years. The breaks coincide with the
release, do not appear under a pandemic placebo test, and hold under a
common censoring rule and occupation-specific pre-shock trends
(unverified: exact regression specification and full robustness-table
contents, beyond what the abstract states).

## Why it matters

Gives economists a quantified, falsifiable account of how generative AI is
repricing human capital within exposed occupations — not just whether AI
affects wages, but *which* component (codified knowledge vs. practice-based
experience) is being repriced, and over what horizon. The event-study
design (pre/post ChatGPT, exposure-quartile comparison, placebo test) is a
directly reusable template for similar labour-economics studies of AI's
effects on wage structure.

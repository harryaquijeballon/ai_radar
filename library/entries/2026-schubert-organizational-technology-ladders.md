# Organizational Technology Ladders: Remote Work and Generative AI Adoption

## Summary
Proposes that organizations climb a "technology ladder," where adopting one technology reshapes hiring and work processes in ways that speed adoption of the next. Using U.S. job-posting data, the paper studies whether remote-work adoption during COVID-19 predicts later generative-AI uptake. A 10-percentage-point increase in remote hiring is associated with a 0.4-percentage-point rise in job postings mentioning generative AI. The proposed mechanism is a shift toward technical and managerial talent through remote hiring; firms with return-to-office mandates show a substantially larger response, which the paper reads as organizational adoption barriers paradoxically sharpening the effect.

## Why it matters
A concrete, quantified link between two waves of workplace technology adoption (remote work, then generative AI) via a stated channel (hiring composition) rather than an assumed one. Gives digital-economy and labour-market researchers a testable "technology ladder" framework and an effect size to compare against, and a specific claim about return-to-office mandates worth checking against other firm-level AI-adoption data.

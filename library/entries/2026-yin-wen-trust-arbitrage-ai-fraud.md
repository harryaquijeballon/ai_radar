# When Trust Attracts Fraud: AI and Trust Arbitrage

## Summary

The authors present a two-market signaling model of fraud in the presence of
generative AI. Their central claim, quoted directly from the abstract:
"Trust can attract fraud when it delays verification." Because AI lowers
both fabrication and verification costs, fraudulent claims can become
profitable before verification occurs. Across markets of differing initial
quality, higher-quality markets verify claims more slowly, creating a window
in which deceptive sellers preferentially target the higher-quality,
less-vigilant market rather than the lower-quality one. This temporarily
erodes the high-quality market's reliability advantage, but the resulting
inflow of fraud triggers verification and deters further entry — a
self-regulating dynamic the authors term "trust arbitrage."

## Why it matters

The paper offers a specific, falsifiable mechanism — rather than a general
warning — for how AI-driven verification-cost reductions redistribute fraud
across markets by quality tier, and why that redistribution is
self-correcting rather than permanent. That is a usable input for framing
fraud-related AI regulation and verification-timing policy: it predicts
*where* fraud concentrates first (higher-quality, slower-to-verify markets)
and *why* it recedes without additional intervention, which changes what a
policy response should target (accelerating verification in high-quality
markets) versus what it need not (permanent quality-market intervention).

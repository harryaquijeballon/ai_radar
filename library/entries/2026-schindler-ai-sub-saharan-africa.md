# Unlocking the Potential: AI in Sub-Saharan Africa

## Summary
IMF Departmental Paper (DP 2026/013, published 2026-07-13) on AI adoption in sub-Saharan Africa. Per the paper's abstract: AI is emerging as a general-purpose technology with the potential to reshape productivity, labor markets, and growth trajectories worldwide; for sub-Saharan Africa the central concern is not the risk of technological disruption but whether countries can adopt, adapt, and scale AI quickly enough to capture its benefits and avoid falling further behind. The paper applies recent AI-exposure and productivity methods to the region, combining country-level estimates with adoption scenarios and evidence from emerging African use cases. Press coverage reports a headline scenario in which AI could raise sub-Saharan African GDP by around 4 percent, conditional on overcoming infrastructure barriers such as electricity and internet access (unverified — secondary press only, not traced to the paper).

## Why it matters
Most AI-exposure and AI-productivity estimates are built on advanced-economy task data; this paper transplants those methods to a developing region where the binding constraints (connectivity, electricity, skills) differ fundamentally. For researchers on technology and development, it offers citable country-level exposure estimates and adoption scenarios for sub-Saharan Africa, and a framing — adoption speed rather than labor-market disruption as the first-order concern — that changes how AI-and-development policy questions are argued. Directly relevant to connectivity-and-development analysis, since infrastructure access is cast as the gating factor for any productivity gains.

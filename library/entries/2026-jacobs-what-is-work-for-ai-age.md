# What is Work For? Labor, Politics, and Meaning in the Age of AI

## Summary

Julian Jacobs reports an original cross-national survey of 8,234 working-age
adults in the UK, US, Denmark, and India, examining what work provides
beyond income. 81% of respondents report job satisfaction, and a majority in
all four countries say they are strongly motivated by non-monetary aspects
of work. Using a "Work Values" index, the paper finds about one third of
working respondents are intrinsically motivated, about one quarter driven
mainly by social/community aspects, 17% by social status, and just over one
quarter by financial benefit. A majority would leave their job for a
guaranteed income equal to their current salary, but about one fifth would
stay even at 120% of salary; intrinsically motivated workers were
disproportionately likely to reject a basic income at any substitution
level. Workers attached to their jobs for non-monetary reasons were more
likely to pursue volunteering or work-like activity if a guaranteed income
replaced their role. These motivations are evenly distributed across income
levels and between white- and blue-collar workers. The author argues a basic
income may be needed to manage a future AI labour shock but may not
compensate workers for the lost non-monetary benefits of work.

## Why it matters

Supplies a quantified, falsifiable framework (the Work Values index) for a
debate — whether basic income can substitute for work displaced by AI —
that is otherwise argued mostly on intuition; directly usable by researchers
and policymakers assessing UBI-style responses to AI-driven labour
disruption.

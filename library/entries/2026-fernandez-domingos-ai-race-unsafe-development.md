# Falling Behind Drives Unsafe Development in an Idealised AI Race Experiment

## Summary

A behavioural experiment testing what drives unsafe development choices in
an idealised AI race. Participants in paired races repeatedly chose between
safe and unsafe development paths under uncertain timelines; unsafe choices
gave faster progress at accumulated risk levels of 10%, 60%, or 90%. The
authors report that "unsafe development can emerge from early behavioural
momentum, opponent behaviour, and fear of falling behind, rather than from
risk preferences alone," and that participants trailing an opponent were
more likely to choose unsafe paths regardless of their individual risk
tolerance. An evolutionary model with four strategic archetypes is used to
explain how conditional unsafe behaviour becomes favoured under competitive
dynamics. The paper concludes that policy should prioritise "reducing
competitive pressure and promoting cooperation in AI development rather
than only individual risk" management.

## Why it matters

A concrete, quantified challenge to policy framings that address AI-safety
risk purely through individual-lab risk tolerance or compliance
requirements: this experiment locates the causal driver in relative
competitive position, meaning safety interventions aimed only at
individual actors' risk appetite may leave the "falling behind" incentive
untouched. Directly usable for anyone evaluating or designing AI-race
policy responses (e.g., coordination mechanisms, information-sharing on
relative position) rather than only individual-firm safety mandates.

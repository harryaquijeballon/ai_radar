# Emerging Challenges in Threat Modeling for GenAI-Augmented Systems: A View from the Trenches

## Summary
The paper reports findings from applying three threat modeling techniques to an industrial case study of a Generative AI-augmented system, arguing that traditional threat modeling methods such as STRIDE are insufficient to assess emerging GenAI-specific risks. It identifies significant gaps in existing methods' support for GenAI-specific risk categories, particularly around software supply chains and human-centered security issues, and documents practitioner perspectives on adopting these methods within SME (small/medium enterprise) development workflows.

## Why it matters
For security engineers applying conventional threat-modeling frameworks to GenAI-augmented systems, this is a grounded warning that established methods (STRIDE and similar) leave specific gap categories — supply-chain and human-centered risks — unaddressed, based on a real industrial case study rather than a generic risk taxonomy. It is a useful diagnostic input for anyone deciding whether to extend or supplement STRIDE-style threat modeling for GenAI components, though the paper documents gaps rather than proposing a validated new method.

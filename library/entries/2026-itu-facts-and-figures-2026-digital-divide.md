# Facts and Figures 2026: 6.3 Billion People Now Online, Yet Digital Divides Persist

## Summary

ITU's annual *Facts and Figures* report estimates that 6.3 billion people
(76% of the world's population) used the internet in 2026, up from a
revised 6.1 billion (74%) in 2025; about 2 billion people remain offline.
Reported gaps: internet use is 93% in high-income countries versus 32% in
low-income countries; 79% of men versus 74% of women use the internet
(female-to-male ratio 0.94); 86% of 15-24-year-olds versus 74% of everyone
else; 82% of urban residents versus 63% of rural residents. Mobile-phone
ownership (age 10+) is 84% globally, exceeding 95% in high-income countries
and 57% in low-income countries. Mobile network coverage: 97% of the
population is within reach of at least 3G; 5G reaches 59% globally, ranging
from 86% (high-income) to 4% (low-income countries). Average monthly mobile
broadband data use is 20GB in high-income countries versus 3.4GB in
low-income countries. The global median price of a data-only mobile
broadband basket fell to 1.2% of GNI per capita (fixed broadband: 2.3%);
landlocked developing countries and small island developing states met the
2%-of-GNI mobile affordability target for the first time in 2026, while
least-developed countries' median mobile broadband price remains 6.1% of
GNI per capita and three in ten economies meet neither affordability
target. ITU explicitly frames connectivity, affordability, and digital
skills as determining who benefits from AI, and warns that the digital
divide could widen in the AI era without expanded meaningful connectivity.

## Why it matters

The authoritative, citable baseline for global connectivity and the
digital divide, refreshed annually: specific adoption, coverage,
affordability, and demographic-gap figures (income, gender, age,
urban/rural) that a researcher can use directly in any analysis of
technology diffusion, digital inclusion, or AI-era inequality, rather than
estimating or citing stale figures. The report's own framing — that
affordability and skills gate who benefits from AI — gives a direct,
citable link between this connectivity data and AI-and-development
arguments (lens 2).

# AI-Mediation in Retail Markets: Revenue Effects and Assortment Optimization

## Summary

The paper studies how a retailer should choose its assortment when an AI shopping assistant decides which products a customer sees, and the customer then chooses from that shortlist under a multinomial logit model. Two AI behaviours are modelled: deterministic mediation (fixed ranking per customer segment) and stochastic mediation (Plackett-Luce). Per the arXiv abstract page, the authors build a dataset of product rankings from leading AI models, fit Plackett-Luce models and test them on held-out assortments. With synthetic customer preferences, ignoring AI mediation costs mean revenue of 2.52% to 11.37% relative to the Plackett-Luce optimum depending on capacity configuration; a deterministic plug-in using only the ordering of fitted AI utilities loses at most 0.53%; under deterministic mediation with a fixed number of segments, assortment optimization has an FPTAS.

## Why it matters

A quantified, modelled channel through which AI shopping agents change seller outcomes, usable as a framework for competition and platform analysis. Revenue figures rest on synthetic customer preferences, so they are model-based magnitudes, not observed market effects.

# Near-Daily AI Use Among US Adults Has More Than Doubled in Six Months

## Summary
Epoch AI, using two Ipsos KnowledgePanel surveys of US adults (March 3-5,
2026, n=2,017; August 28-30, 2026, n=1,016), finds that the share of US
adults using AI near-daily (6-7 days/week) rose from 8% in March 2026 to 19%
in August 2026 — more than doubling in six months. Over the same period,
single-day-per-week usage declined from 17% to 10%, overall AI adoption rose
from 50% to 56.4%, and moderate usage (2-5 days/week) rose slightly from
24.3% to 27.3%. Results are weighted to the US adult population with 90%
Wilson confidence intervals reported.

The authors flag a methodology change between waves as a limitation: the
March survey asked respondents to select from three preset frequency bands,
while the August survey asked for exact per-service daily counts (with the
maximum taken across services). This could affect comparability — the August
service-specific questioning may understate frequency, while exact day counts
might encourage rounding to salient numbers "(unverified how large this
effect is)."

## Why it matters
This is a rare like-for-like, short-interval (six-month) read on the
intensive margin of AI adoption — not just whether people have tried AI, but
how habitual its use has become. For growth and productivity researchers
(lens 1), the near-daily-use share is a more economically meaningful
diffusion metric than headline "ever used" adoption rates, since productivity
effects plausibly concentrate among frequent users. The stated methodology
change between waves is an important caveat for anyone citing the doubling
figure as a clean trend.

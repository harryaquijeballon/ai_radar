# Shopping by Algorithm: How Agentic AI Deploys Human Heuristics as a Surrogate Consumer

## Summary

Using a "Tool-Lab" methodology, the authors test eight LLMs across three
commercial providers as autonomous shopping agents exposed to marketing
tactics such as just-below pricing and promotional framing. Under zero-cost
information conditions, these pricing cues had "minimal misleading effect."
When information acquisition carried a cost and user instructions were
vague, the agents began exhibiting what the authors call "search-mediated
vulnerability" — omitting attributes needed for an optimal choice, in a
pattern resembling human decision-making shortcuts. More specific user
instructions "largely preserved optimal decision-making despite cost
constraints."

## Why it matters

As consumers increasingly delegate purchasing decisions to AI agents,
platforms and regulators need evidence on whether classic manipulative
design (dark patterns, pricing psychology) still works against a
non-human buyer. This experiment gives a quantified, conditional answer:
manipulation is largely ineffective when information is free, but a
realistic combination of cost and vague instructions reopens the same
vulnerability humans have — with a concrete mitigation (specific
instructions) available to the user delegating the purchase. That
conditionality is a usable input for platform-design and
consumer-protection analysis, not just a general caution.

# The AI Expectations Gap

## Summary

A report from The Anthropic Institute comparing expectations about AI's future between a nationally representative sample of ~11,000 US adults (surveyed by Morning Consult in August 2026) and Anthropic staff. Both groups broadly agree on today's capabilities but diverge sharply on pace and personal impact: 39% of US adults vs. 91% of staff expect AI to produce a Nobel-level discovery by 2030. Staff expect AI to be able to do 75% of their current work by 2030 on average; US workers expect 28%, with only about 5% expecting 80% or more. US adults expect 42% of automatable tasks to actually be done by AI by 2030; staff expect 65%. Median implied 2030 GDP growth built from these task-automation expectations: 5.4% for US adults vs. 17.1% for staff (and 12.5% for a separate online-quiz sample). US workers who expect 80%+ of their own work to be automated forecast 12.2% unemployment in 2030, against an assumed 4.2% current rate. Staff's median estimate of AI's own share of frontier-model development work: 51% today, rising to 80% by end-2027 and 95% by 2030. The report builds its GDP-growth translation on the model in the companion working paper "Economic Scenarios for Transformative AI" (Korinek, Jones, Sacher, Cotter, McCrory — Anthropic Institute WP 2026-02), and is one of two reports released the same day alongside "How Employment Is Changing at Anthropic" (see Related entries), introduced together in a short framing post, "Working at the Frontier Shapes Expectations for the Future" (anthropic.com/research/expectations-for-the-future).

## Why it matters

Gives economists a quantified anchor for how far public expectations about AI-driven growth lag the expectations of people working at the capability frontier — directly usable as a sensitivity range in growth-scenario work (5.4% vs. 17.1% implied 2030 GDP growth from the same automation-share-to-growth model), and as a specific data point for the debate on whether survey-elicited AI expectations are a leading or lagging indicator of realised growth.

## Verification notes

Fetched and read directly (not abstract-only); all figures above are as stated on the page itself, including author list, institutional citation (The Anthropic Institute), and publication date (16 October 2026). Not independently corroborated against a second source — this is the sole publisher of the underlying survey data. **Vendor/first-party caveat (load-bearing):** Anthropic staff are not a random sample of knowledge workers; the "staff" side of every comparison describes the expectations of people employed by a frontier AI lab, who have unusually direct and continuous exposure to frontier capability, not a generic high-skill benchmark population. The GDP-growth translation (5.4% / 17.1% / 12.5%) is itself a modelled implication of the survey's task-automation answers via the authors' own growth model, not an independent GDP forecast.

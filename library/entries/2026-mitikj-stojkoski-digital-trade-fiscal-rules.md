# The potential impact of fiscal rule changes on digital products trade

## Summary

Following the March 2026 expiry of the WTO moratorium on customs duties for
electronic transmissions, governments can now tax cross-border digital
products with tariffs in addition to existing digital services taxes (DSTs).
The authors combine bilateral trade data for 31 digital-product categories
(2016–2024) with DST rates and tariff equivalents on physical counterparts,
estimate gravity models, and simulate alternative tax regimes. They find
digital trade is lower in destinations with a DST, though the drop is
temporary after adoption within those destinations. In 2024-based scenarios,
adding tariff equivalents on top of existing DSTs would raise revenue from
roughly USD 2.7 billion to USD 6.9 billion while reducing trade by about
1.4%; replacing DSTs with tariff equivalents entirely would raise about USD
4.3 billion. Tariff payments are attributed mainly to the United States,
while DST payments fall mainly on subsidiary locations such as Ireland. The
authors conclude tariffs could raise more revenue than DSTs at a modest
trade cost.

## Why it matters

A quantified, policy-ready comparison of two live fiscal tools for taxing
digital trade, arriving just as the WTO e-commerce moratorium's expiry
reopens the tariff option for governments — useful context for lens 3/4
digital-market-regulation analysis, even though the paper is not AI-specific
(it covers digital products broadly, including but not limited to AI
services).

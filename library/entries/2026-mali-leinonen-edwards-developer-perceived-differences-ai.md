# Perceived Differences in Activity and Experience Between Developing Software With and Without AI

## Summary

This paper (Rubash Mali, Juho Leinonen, John Edwards) studies how software
engineers perceive their activity and experience to differ when developing
with generative AI tools versus traditional development, and how years of
programming experience shapes those perceived differences. Engineers
reported less discussing technical problems with peers, less breaking
problems into parts, less searching documentation, and less using a
debugger when working with AI, alongside more task switching. On the
experience side, engineers reported less deep focus and a reduced sense of
control over code and design. Core coding tasks — writing, refactoring, and
fixing code — showed only small mean differences between the two modes. The
differences were larger for engineers who started programming before
generative AI tools existed, compared to those who started afterward.

## Why it matters

A concrete, if self-reported, signal for engineering-team practice: AI
assistance appears to change *process* (less peer discussion, less
decomposition, less documentation lookup, more switching) more than it
changes raw coding output, and the disruption is unevenly distributed by
when an engineer learned to program — relevant to anyone designing
AI-assisted development workflows or onboarding practices.

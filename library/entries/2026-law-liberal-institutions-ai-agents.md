# Liberal Institutions Are Dead / Long Live Liberal Institutions

## Summary
Law argues liberal institutions (courts, regulators, ombuds bodies) have
long depended on "costly effort" — the time or skill a person must expend to
apply, appeal, object, request, or complain — to keep claim volumes
manageable. AI agents lower that cost: "Agents reduce the cost of doing,
whether it be legitimate assistance or arbitrage, allowing familiar actions
to play out at far greater scale," via delegation in which "a principal
issues instructions and an agent acts on them without supervision." As
submission volumes rise and individual applications grow longer and more
detailed, "organizational performance eventually declines as backlogs swell
and response times lengthen." The essay cites Australia's Fair Work
Commission projecting a 70% workload increase within three years, linked to
AI tool use (unverified beyond the essay's citation). Law's conclusion is
not that liberalism is finished but that institutions "must find a new
settlement under which citizens can participate in liberal society," one
that preserves "equal access, contestability, minimal necessary information,
and plural authority" while navigating trade-offs between expanding
capacity, redesigning process, or restricting access.

## Why it matters
Gives political-science and AI-governance readers a specific causal
mechanism — participation-cost collapse, not just "more AI use" — for why
AI agents strain institutions built around scarce, effortful human
engagement, plus a concrete metric (a named regulator's own workload
projection) to anchor further empirical work on institutional capacity
under AI-driven demand.

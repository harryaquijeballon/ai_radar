# Stranded credentials: how a skill-signaling market absorbed generative AI

## Summary

The paper investigates whether professional credentials maintain their
predictive value for job performance during the generative-AI era, using
Kaggle competition data spanning 2010-2026 across more than 444,000
participations in two evaluation formats. Recent credentials retain most
of their signaling value through the AI transition, but older credential
stocks became substantially less informative: "upload-competition medal
stocks lost 82% of their informativeness," with institutional factors
(rather than AI capability growth alone) explaining half to three-quarters
of the decline. The author concludes that "credentials are informative,
perishable, institution-bound, and interdependent," framing credential
value under AI as an institutional design problem (unverified in detail —
the identification strategy separating institutional from AI-capability
effects not read beyond the abstract).

## Why it matters

A concrete, quantified natural-experiment test of a widely asserted but
rarely measured claim — that generative AI erodes the value of
credentials as labour-market signals — with a striking finding that most
of the erosion traces to institutional factors, not AI capability itself.
Directly usable for researchers studying AI's effects on labour-market
signaling, human-capital measurement, or credentialing-system design.

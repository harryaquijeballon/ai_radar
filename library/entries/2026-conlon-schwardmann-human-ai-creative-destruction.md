# Creative destruction: Human-AI competition in an experimental market

## Summary

Per the arXiv abstract page (submitted 2026-10-12, econ.GN), the paper tests two routes by which humans might keep a role in production as AI improves: a lasting edge in taste, or consumer preference for human involvement. Three studies with 1,025 participants in total had amateurs and professional creatives design computer games with an AI coding agent and had consumers decide whether to buy games, with and without disclosure that a game was human-designed. The authors report no evidence for either hypothesis: games designed by AI alone outperformed those from human producers, including professionals; human producers did not anticipate the AI's advantage; and telling consumers a game was human-designed did not raise willingness to pay.

## Why it matters

Experimental design reference for studying human-AI substitution with consumer-side demand measured, relevant to debates about whether a "human premium" protects creative work. Limited generalisability: one product type, participants' own tooling, a single paper.

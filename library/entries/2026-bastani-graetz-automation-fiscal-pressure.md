# AI, Automation, and Fiscal Pressure

## Summary

The paper studies how task-level automation affects public finances in
countries that tax labor more heavily than capital and fund public services
(health care, education) from general tax revenue rather than user fees. The
authors build a two-sector general-equilibrium model with task-level
automation, calibrated to EU data. Automation shifts income toward capital,
which is taxed more lightly, eroding the tax base. If public-service
provision tracks consumer demand, the budget deficit widens at unchanged tax
rates — even when the public sector automates exactly as fast as the private
sector. In the EU-27 benchmark, where market-sector automation is faster,
the deficit rises by almost one percentage point of value added, despite the
economy growing about 12% from automation.

## Why it matters

A concrete, calibrated counterweight to the assumption that AI-driven growth
automatically eases public-finance pressure: growth and fiscal sustainability
can move in opposite directions once the tax base shifts from labor to
lightly-taxed capital. Usable directly as a modeling framework or a
sensitivity check by anyone evaluating AI-growth scenarios against public
budgets (lens 1), and as evidence for the policy argument that tax-base
composition, not just growth, has to be addressed by AI-era fiscal policy
(lens 4).

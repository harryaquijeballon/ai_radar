# Taxonomy-Driven Analysis of Open-Source AI Risk Mitigation Tools

## Summary

Addresses fragmentation in AI risk-mitigation tooling by proposing a
structured methodology for evaluating open-source solutions. The authors
examined 21 tools against the extended MIT AI Risk Mitigation taxonomy's 32
subcategories, using a retrieval-augmented-generation pipeline to map tool
capabilities to taxonomy subcategories (75.5% F1 for the mapping). The
analysis finds significant imbalance in coverage: tools cluster around
technical and operational controls, while governance, legal and
regulatory, and financial and market controls remain largely unaddressed —
evidence that comprehensive AI risk management requires combining
automated tooling with organizational process and human oversight.

## Why it matters

A reference map for teams choosing open-source AI governance and
risk-mitigation tooling: it names the taxonomy gap (governance,
legal/regulatory, financial/market controls are underserved by current
open-source tools) rather than leaving teams to discover it by trial and
error, and is a useful starting point before assuming a purely tooling-based
approach to AI risk management is sufficient.

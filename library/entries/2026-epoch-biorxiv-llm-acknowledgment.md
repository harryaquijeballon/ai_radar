# LLM use is acknowledged almost as often as specialized AI tools in biology preprints

## Summary

Epoch AI examined every bioRxiv preprint from January 2020 through September
2026, using an AI classifier (GPT-5.6 Sol) to detect acknowledgments of LLM
use and of specialized AI-tool use, counted separately, plus a second
classifier (OpenAI's Decisions API, based on GPT-6 Luna) to sort LLM
acknowledgments into four use cases: research, code/computation, writing,
and literature review. The share of preprints acknowledging LLM use has
grown exponentially since 2023 and has nearly caught up with acknowledgments
of specialized AI-tool use: 17% versus 19% as of the most recent data. About
4% of September 2026 preprints acknowledged LLM use specifically for
research or analysis, up from about 1% at the start of 2026. AlphaFold
remains the single most commonly acknowledged specialized tool, appearing in
about half of preprints that acknowledge any specialized AI tool; LLM use is
more varied, spanning literature review, writing, and coding assistance. The
authors compiled a list of 150 specialized tools by sampling preprints and
scanning for tool mentions.

## Why it matters

A transparent example of using AI classifiers themselves to measure AI-tool
adoption in research practice at scale — relevant context for anyone
tracking how disclosure norms (not just true usage) are evolving in
AI-assisted research, with explicit caveats about what voluntary
acknowledgment can and cannot tell you.

## Verification notes

Read directly from the Epoch AI data-insight page
(epoch.ai/data-insights/biorxiv-llms-vs-ai-tools, dated 30 Oct 2026). The
17%/19% acknowledgment figures, the 4%/1% research-use figures, and the
AlphaFold prevalence claim are traced to the page's stated methodology and
results. The authors' own stated limitations (voluntary-acknowledgment bias,
AI-model measurement error in both classifiers, an incomplete 150-tool list)
are carried into this entry rather than smoothed over.

# Will Huawei Catch Up to Nvidia by 2030?

## Summary

Epoch AI models whether Huawei can close the AI-chip gap with Nvidia by 2030
and concludes it is unlikely to, primarily because of high-bandwidth memory
(HBM) supply constraints created by export controls. Reported figures: Huawei
produced an estimated 1.5 million AI chip units in 2026 (about 880,000
H100-equivalents) versus Nvidia's 5.9 million units (about 23 million
H100-equivalents) — under 4% of Nvidia's compute output; without foreign
memory access, that share could fall to roughly 1% by 2028. Nvidia's B300 is
estimated to deliver about 6.75x more FP4 performance than Huawei's Ascend
950. The analysis reports a 73x gap in usable HBM between the two companies
in 2026, narrowing to 11x by 2028 as Chinese HBM producers (CXMT, XMC) scale
domestic wafer capacity, and estimates TSMC holds roughly 2x the transistor
density of SMIC through 2030. Huawei's long-term bet, "LogicFolding" (stacking
logic dies vertically) to work around density limits, is not expected to
reach Ascend chips until 2030 — two years after Nvidia's projected Feynman
architecture. Methodology: volume projections from HBM wafer-capacity data,
performance comparisons in H100-equivalents, and a supply-chain decomposition
across logic fabrication, memory sourcing (domestic, stockpiled, smuggled)
and packaging, under three memory-supply scenarios.

## Why it matters

Gives economists and policy researchers assessing AI-chip export-control
policy a quantified, transparent counterpoint to qualitative "China is
catching up" narratives: a specific, falsifiable estimate of how far behind
Huawei's production capacity and per-chip performance remain, and which
single input (HBM supply) is the binding constraint. Useful as an evidence
base for competition/regulation analysis of AI compute markets and for
evaluating the practical effect of current export-control regimes.

# AI, Human Cognition and Knowledge Collapse

## Summary

Acemoglu, Kong and Ozdaglar (Feb 2026, 68 pp.) build a dynamic model of learning where good decisions require both community-level *general* knowledge and individual *context-specific* knowledge, as complements. Human learning effort produces both a private signal and a public one that accumulates into collective knowledge (a learning externality); agentic AI supplies context-specific recommendations that *substitute* for that effort. Central result: when human effort is sufficiently elastic and agentic recommendations exceed an accuracy threshold, the economy can tip into a **knowledge-collapse steady state** — general knowledge vanishes even as personalized AI advice stays high-quality. Welfare is non-monotone in agentic accuracy, implying an interior welfare-maximizing level of agentic precision and motivating information-design regulation; by contrast, better aggregation of human-generated knowledge unambiguously raises welfare and resilience. The paper cites early empirical patterns consistent with the mechanism (reduced activity on Stack Overflow and Wikipedia where LLMs substitute).

## Why it matters

*(Radar's assessment.)* The formal backbone for the question your note raised — whether AI expands human knowledge: the model's answer is "it depends on whether AI substitutes for or complements human learning, and the substitution case can be self-undermining at the collective level." Directly usable in policy debates about AI in education and research, and a rigorous grounding for the trust/epistemics lens. For AI builders (scored low, not n/a): the welfare-maximizing-precision result is a rare theory-side argument that *maximally helpful* agentic systems are not automatically socially optimal.

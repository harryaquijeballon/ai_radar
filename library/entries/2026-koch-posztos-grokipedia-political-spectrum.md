# Grokipedia Stretches the US Political Spectrum

## Summary

Grokipedia, an AI-generated encyclopedia built by xAI's model and marketed as
a less-biased alternative to the crowd-edited Wikipedia, is compared against
Wikipedia on biographies of 1,614 current and former US Democratic and
Republican members of Congress (from Andrew Jackson to the sitting
Congress), using snapshots from February and November 2026. For about nine
in ten Republican politicians, Grokipedia's biography reads as if written by
a more conservative author than Wikipedia's version; for Democrats this
holds only about half the time. Among the 1,022 politicians with
Congressional roll-call voting records, the perceived conservatism of the
biography's author rises linearly with the politician's own ideology score,
and this slope is roughly twice as steep on Grokipedia as on Wikipedia. The
authors find that politicians' own ideology — rather than, for instance,
extra bias in races where Elon Musk's America PAC spent money — explains the
partisan asymmetry. Their conclusion: Grokipedia does not uniformly favor
one party; instead it "stretches" the political spectrum, pushing the most
liberal politicians' biographies further left and the most conservative
politicians' further right relative to Wikipedia's framing.

## Why it matters

A directly on-lens empirical result for political science and AI-governance
questions: platform-level editorial choices in an LLM-authored reference
work measurably amplify political polarization in how public figures are
described, via a quantified, ideology-linked mechanism rather than a simple
partisan-skew story. The paired biography-vs-roll-call-ideology comparison
design is itself usable as a method template for auditing other
AI-generated reference or search products for similar framing effects.

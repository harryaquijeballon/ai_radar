# Past Automation and Future AI: How Weak Links Tame the Growth Explosion

## Summary

NBER Working Paper 36050 (Charles I. Jones, Christopher Tonetti) uses
task-based growth accounting to show that capital task productivity in the
US economy has grown about 5 percentage points per year faster than labor
task productivity. The authors argue automation's main benefit is replacing
slowly-improving human labor with rapidly-improving machines on more tasks
over time. They calibrate a growth model in which the production of both
goods and ideas is endogenously automated; in this model growth can
accelerate and even explode, but the acceleration is slowed by "weak links" —
tasks that still require human labor.

## Why it matters

Gives a quantified empirical anchor (a 5-percentage-point-per-year growth
differential between capital and labor task productivity) plus a calibrated
model of how automation and AI could reshape long-run growth rates —
directly usable by anyone building or critiquing AI-growth projections, and
a natural companion to the task-based framework in
[2017-aghion-jones-jones-ai-growth](2017-aghion-jones-jones-ai-growth.md).

# Agentic AI Orchestration of Heterogeneous Economic Models for Rapid, Multi-scenario Analysis of Energy Crises

## Summary

Addresses a timing mismatch: individual economic models take months to
build and validate, but energy-crisis policy decisions must be made within
days. The authors build a framework in which an LLM orchestrates —  but
never itself generates — outputs from 16 existing, heterogeneous models
covering oil, natural gas, shipping, water, helium, fertilizer, and
macroeconomic equilibrium, integrating them across different programming
environments and formats into internally consistent multi-scenario
analyses. The design constrains the LLM's role deliberately: all
quantitative outputs originate from the underlying model runs, each
number remains traceable to its source model, and the process is subject
to analyst approval at each stage, rather than allowing any single model's
assumptions to dominate the combined scenario. The framework was tested on
a live case: the 2026 closure of the Strait of Hormuz, with the
multi-model analysis refreshed weekly over eight weeks as the real-world
situation continued to unfold.

## Why it matters

For the social-science audience: a concrete demonstration that agentic
orchestration can compress the months-to-days gap between model-building
and crisis decision timelines without collapsing model diversity into a
single black box — a template directly usable for economists needing
fast, defensible multi-model synthesis. For the AI-engineering audience: a
transferable reliability pattern for exactly this radar's standing
interest in agentic simulation of policy interventions — orchestration
without generation, per-output traceability to source models, and staged
analyst approval — validated against a real, unfolding crisis rather than
a static benchmark.

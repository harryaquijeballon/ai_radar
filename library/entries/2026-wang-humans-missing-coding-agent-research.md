# Humans are Missing from AI Coding Agent Research

## Summary

The paper argues that as AI coding agents have improved at autonomously
completing software engineering tasks, the practical bottleneck has shifted
away from raw task-solving capability and toward how users communicate with,
supervise, and trust these agents. The authors propose redesigning coding
agent systems around human-agent collaboration rather than pure autonomy,
and organize this around four interaction-level dimensions: task alignment,
verifiability, steerability, and adaptability. They propose research
directions including user-involved coding environments, stronger
verification mechanisms, and principled measures of human-agent interaction
quality. (unverified: no quantitative results are given — this is a
position/research-agenda paper, not an empirical study.)

## Why it matters

Offers ai-engineering builders a named vocabulary — task alignment,
verifiability, steerability, adaptability — for auditing whether a coding
agent product is actually usable by its human operators, not just capable in
benchmark isolation. Directly on lens 7 (AI-assisted software development)
and adjacent to lens 4 (evaluation/validation), since it argues current
benchmarks miss the interaction dimension entirely.

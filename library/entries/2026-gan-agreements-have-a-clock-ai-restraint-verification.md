# Agreements Have a Clock: Compute Growth and the Verification of AI Restraint

## Summary

Asks what a frontier-AI pause or restraint agreement would need to be
sustainable, drawing on past arms-control, ozone, and emissions agreements.
Argues cooperation holds only while the monitoring needed to deter a secret
breakout costs no more than either side gains from cooperating, and that
compute growth during a pause erodes this over time: a secret advance
reaches the frozen frontier sooner as the pause continues, shortening the
window for a credible response. Builds a formal model (voluntary entry,
costly monitoring, secret advances, credible retaliation) in which a follower
advances through its own research plus diffusion from the paused leader,
with diffusion weakening as the technology gap narrows — so a paused leader
keeps its lead longer than its raw head start implies. In a worked example
with a fixed leader window: cooperation holds through month 18.9; adding
verified, matched safety research extends this to month 25.5; halving the
monitoring cost extends it further to month 31.8.

## Why it matters

Gives a concrete, quantified framework for a question any AI-pause or
restraint policy proposal must answer — not "should labs agree to slow
down" but "for how long can such an agreement actually hold, given that
compute keeps growing and someone has to pay for verification." The finding
that monitoring-cost reductions and safety-research reciprocity measurably
extend an agreement's sustainable lifespan is directly usable by anyone
designing or critiquing a compute-governance or AI-pause proposal.

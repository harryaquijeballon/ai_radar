# Vibe Coding: Practice, Performance, Productivity, and Risk - A State-of-the-Art Review

## Summary

The paper synthesizes empirical evidence across a cross-disciplinary corpus on "vibe coding" — AI-assisted development where the developer describes intent in natural language and validates results by running rather than reading the generated code. It reports conflicting productivity findings across studies, ranging from a 26% improvement to a 19% slowdown depending on measurement approach, alongside uneven task-level capability, security vulnerabilities found in deployed vibe-coded applications, and skill-degradation concerns. The authors propose that performance gains may be concentrated in new code while diminishing on established systems — a hypothesis offered to explain the inconsistency in the productivity literature.

## Why it matters

A single reference point for the fragmented and sometimes contradictory vibe-coding productivity literature (lens 7): rather than one more individual study, it maps where the evidence agrees (uneven task-level capability, real security and skill-degradation risks) and where it doesn't (net productivity effect), plus a specific, testable hypothesis — new-code versus legacy-code performance gap — for why studies disagree, which is directly useful for interpreting future single-study claims in this space.

## Verification notes

Read via the arXiv abstract page, which states the review's scope, the productivity-range figures, and the new-vs-legacy-code hypothesis directly. Not independently corroborated against a second source or the full paper; as a synthesis paper, its claims are themselves secondary summaries of other studies rather than primary findings.

# Rule-Based Pricing Algorithms and Market Outcomes: An Experimental Study

## Summary

"Rule-based pricing tools are widespread in digital commerce, yet we know
little about how their design shapes market outcomes." In a controlled
market experiment, participants used dashboards to build pricing algorithms
competing in a sequential Bertrand game over multiple periods. The authors
varied design features found in commercial repricing tools: warnings about
price wars, pre-configured strategies, and advice from a large language
model. "Most treatment variations raise market prices with effects driven by
an increase in starting prices and more cooperative algorithm designs." The
authors state directly that "the results matter for competition policy,
platform regulation and current discussions on regulating algorithm design
tools."

## Why it matters

Regulators examining whether algorithmic-pricing tools facilitate tacit
collusion get a specific, causal answer about *which design choices* matter
(starting-price defaults, pre-configured cooperative strategies, and —
notably — LLM-generated advice) rather than a generic concern about
"algorithms and collusion." That specificity is directly usable in framing
what a regulation of algorithmic pricing-tool design should target.

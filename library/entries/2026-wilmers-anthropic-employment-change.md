# How Employment Is Changing at Anthropic

## Summary

A technical report from The Anthropic Institute using the company's own internal hiring, time-use, and output data as what the authors frame as an early indicator for the wider economy. Key figures as stated on the page: the share of junior-level employees in technical departments fell from 21% to 14% since early 2025, and the share of junior technical *hires* fell from 27% to 9%. The technical share of total employment fell from 61% to 56% over the past year while sales, finance, and recruiting grew faster. AI agents' work-hours passed human work-hours in March 2026, and by August agents worked about six times as many hours as their human handlers. Code commits per engineer reached roughly 5x their level six months earlier; comparing engineers who adopted coding agents against those who did not, the authors report no measurable increase in code later removed or reverted. About 80% of interactive agent-session time is spent waiting for human input, and the authors estimate that reading all interactive Claude output for non-technical staff would take over half a work week. The share of interactive sessions focused on managing agents (rather than other work) rose from 10% (fall 2025) to 22% (spring 2026). Mentions of candidates' AI-tool use in hiring materials tripled for technical roles and quadrupled for non-technical roles since 2024, with growing emphasis on autonomy/judgment and declining emphasis on domain expertise. Median employee meeting time rose 20% over the past year. The authors recommend broad employee stock ownership, an early-warning system for at-risk jobs, and internal reallocation, but state plainly that these do not address the decline in junior hiring, which they say requires government policy on retraining and income support. Released the same day as the companion survey report "The AI Expectations Gap" (see Related entries), under a shared framing post, "Working at the Frontier Shapes Expectations for the Future" (anthropic.com/research/expectations-for-the-future).

## Why it matters

For social-science readers: a rare *realised* (not survey-elicited) firm-level dataset on how AI-agent adoption reshapes hiring composition, junior-role entry, and time allocation — a concrete empirical input for the junior-labour-market-displacement debate, with the authors' own policy conclusion that firm-level mitigation (stock ownership, reallocation) cannot substitute for public retraining/income-support policy.

For AI-engineering readers: a quantified, named-company data point on coding-agent adoption at scale — 5x commit throughput with no detected rise in reverted code is a directly citable counter to "agents increase rework" concerns, and the 80%-of-session-time-waiting-for-humans figure is a concrete measurement of where the human bottleneck in agentic SDLC workflows now sits.

## Verification notes

Fetched and read directly (not abstract-only); all figures above are as stated on the page, including author list, institutional citation (The Anthropic Institute, Technical Report), and publication date (16 October 2026). Not independently corroborated against a second source. **First-party, n=1-firm caveat (load-bearing):** every figure describes a single company — a frontier AI lab that is simultaneously the maker of the agents being measured, operating under unusual hiring, compensation, and workload conditions. None of it should be read as a representative estimate for the wider economy or software industry without that caveat; the authors themselves frame it only as an "early indicator," not a representative one. The "no measurable increase in reverted code" claim is an internal comparison described only at the level the page states it (adopters vs. non-adopters of coding agents); the comparison's controls and statistical method are not given on this page and were not independently checked.

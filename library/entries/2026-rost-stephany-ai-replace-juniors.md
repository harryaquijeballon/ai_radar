# Will AI Replace Juniors? How Task Complexity Reduces Automation Risk

## Summary

Vincent Rost and Fabian Stephany ran a pre-registered survey experiment with
managers at 1,435 German firms in the information economy and manufacturing.
Managers were randomly asked to consider a long-lasting need for extra
capacity, involving either well-structured, standardized tasks or more
complex tasks requiring professional judgement, contextual knowledge, and
independent problem-solving. Compared with standardized tasks, complex tasks
reduced managers' willingness to use AI instead of hiring an entry-level
employee by 0.54 points on a five-point scale, and raised the perceived
importance of human oversight by 0.15 points. Before treatment, managers
expected AI availability to reduce hiring across both entry-level and
experienced workers, but expected a significantly larger reduction for
entry-level workers.

## Why it matters

Direct, quantified evidence that task complexity — not just seniority — is
the variable that protects junior workers from AI-driven hiring
substitution, with effect sizes a labor economist or workforce planner could
cite directly. The baseline finding (managers expect larger hiring cuts for
entry-level than experienced workers from AI) also quantifies a bias that
complex-task framing only partially offsets, relevant to debates about
AI-driven labor-market polarization by seniority (lens 1).

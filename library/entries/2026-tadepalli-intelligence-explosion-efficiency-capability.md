# Efficiency vs Capability in the Intelligence Explosion

## Summary

Argues AI progress affects AI research through two distinct channels:
capability gains let AI systems take on a larger share of research tasks,
and efficiency gains raise research output per unit of compute on tasks
already automated. Using a task-automation model, the paper claims that a
"software-driven intelligence explosion" — a self-sustaining acceleration
at fixed compute and labour — requires both channels to improve fast
enough, and that a shortfall in either cannot be made up by the other.
Calibrated to recent AI progress data, capability progress is reported as
fast enough to support such an explosion, while efficiency progress is
"borderline." Monte Carlo simulations reportedly produce an explosion in
49% of draws, with the rate of efficiency progress identified as the
largest source of uncertainty (45% of the uncertainty over whether an
explosion occurs). The author concludes better data on efficiency progress
in frontier models is needed (all figures as stated in the fetched
abstract; full derivation and calibration data not reviewed at capture).

## Why it matters

A usable, quantified framework — rather than a qualitative argument — for
economists and growth researchers evaluating "intelligence explosion" or
recursive-self-improvement claims about AI's effect on the pace of AI
research itself. The explicit two-channel decomposition (capability vs.
efficiency) and the identification of efficiency-progress measurement as
the key uncertainty give a concrete research agenda: what to measure, and
why the capability-only framing common in press coverage is incomplete.

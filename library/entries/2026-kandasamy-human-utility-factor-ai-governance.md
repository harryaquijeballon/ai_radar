# The Human Utility Factor: A Computable Welfare Metric That Reframes AI Governance as a Constrained Optimisation Problem

## Summary
The paper introduces the "Human Utility Factor" (HUF), a mathematical framework proposed to address a gap in current AI governance approaches: existing regulation such as the EU AI Act focuses on safety and transparency but lacks quantitative measures of socioeconomic impact. HUF models three interconnected dimensions — Agency, Wellbeing, and Economic Stability — as functions of policy mechanisms including automation depth, redistribution intensity, and employment coverage. The author reports the framework "yields a closed-form optimal automation level and a minimum redistribution threshold," reframing AI governance as a constrained-optimisation problem rather than a compliance checklist. The model is tested using multi-agent reinforcement learning across U.S., Canadian, and Nordic policy scenarios. A stated key finding: welfare metrics without an explicit redistribution constraint can paradoxically favour high-automation outcomes that technically satisfy the metric while harming broader societal welfare.

## Why it matters
For anyone evaluating or designing AI-and-labour-market policy, this offers a concrete, closed-form analytical object — an optimal automation level and a minimum redistribution threshold — that could be applied or contested directly, rather than a general call for "responsible AI." The finding that unconstrained welfare metrics can rationalise high-automation, low-welfare outcomes is a specific, actionable warning for anyone designing or citing AI-policy welfare metrics.

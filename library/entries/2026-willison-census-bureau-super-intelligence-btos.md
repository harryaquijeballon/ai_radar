# The U.S. Census Bureau and "Super Intelligence"

## Summary

Revisits the U.S. Census Bureau's Business Trends and Outlook Survey
(BTOS), a biweekly survey of roughly 200,000 businesses. The author had
previously charted the series in 2025 and found large firms' reported AI
use appeared to be leveling off; rerunning the analysis with the latest
data, he identifies two discontinuities. First, in November 2025 the
Bureau changed the survey question from AI use "in producing goods or
services" to AI use "in any of its business functions" — answers before
and after are not directly comparable. Under the broader post-change
wording, reported adoption among the largest firms (250+ employees) rose
from about 12% in September 2025 to about 40% by October 2026. Second,
following an executive order, the survey changed terminology in October
2026 to ask about "Super Intelligence or SI" instead of AI; the first
results under this new wording are due for publication soon, and the
author questions whether the new term will confuse respondents (as
restated from the source post; the underlying Census Bureau survey
instrument and microdata were not independently fetched at capture).

## Why it matters

A specific, actionable caution for anyone citing BTOS business-AI-adoption
figures: the series is not a single continuous trend line — a November
2025 wording change already breaks pre/post comparability, and an
October 2026 terminology swap ("Super Intelligence") is about to do so
again. The 12%→40% figure for large-firm adoption looks dramatic but is
partly a measurement artifact of the broadened question, not a clean
behavioral trend — exactly the kind of productivity/adoption-measurement
pitfall this profile's lens 1 tracks. Researchers using BTOS data should
flag both discontinuities rather than report the raw series as a
continuous trend.

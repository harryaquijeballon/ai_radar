# Do People Follow AI Advice? Evidence from a Pension Portfolio Choice Experiment

## Summary
400 employed adults in South Korean workplace pension plans allocated a hypothetical balance across eleven investment products, then could revise after receiving an AI recommendation. A 2x2 design varied recommendation content (aggressive vs. conservative) and whether a rationale accompanied it. About 37% of the experimentally induced difference between aggressive and conservative recommendations passes through to final portfolios; 81% of participants revised after seeing a recommendation, and among revisers 95% shifted toward the assigned recommendation, implementing roughly half the suggested adjustment. Including a rationale produced no measurable effect on uptake. Revisions meaningfully changed portfolio risk exposure but showed no detectable difference in Sharpe ratios.

## Why it matters
A quantified, replicable method for measuring how much weight people give AI advice versus their own priors — the ~37% pass-through and the null result on rationales are concrete findings usable in any research on when trust in AI-generated recommendations is (or isn't) warranted, and specifically relevant to designing or regulating AI-assisted financial-advice tools, where explanation is often assumed to matter for adoption.

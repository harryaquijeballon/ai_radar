# Risks and Controls for Multi-Agent Systems: an analytical framework for deployment of AI agents across organisational boundaries

## Summary
The paper presents "a framework to help organisations, policymakers and
researchers reason about the risks that emerge when AI agents interact with
each other, how those risks change as interactions cross organisational
boundaries, and the controls that may help address them." It establishes
three deployment tiers by governance structure: singular governance (one
organization controls all interacting agents), federated governance
(multiple organizations share a controlled environment), and open
environments (decentralized operation under voluntary standards). For each
tier, the authors work through risk factors, failure modes, available
controls, and identify who is responsible for implementation and where
governance gaps require collective action rather than unilateral fixes.

## Why it matters
A structured way to reason about which agent-interaction risks are solvable
by a single organization's own controls versus which require
cross-organizational coordination or standards — directly usable by anyone
designing governance for agentic systems that will interact with agents
outside their own control, a distinction the profile's governance lens
otherwise leaves implicit.

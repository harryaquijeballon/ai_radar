# The impact of artificial intelligence on output and inflation

## Summary

BIS Working Paper No 1179 (17 April 2024; JEL E31, J24, O33, O40) examines
how AI affects employment, output, and inflation at sector and aggregate
level. The authors build an index of industry exposure to AI and use it to
calibrate a multi-sector macroeconomic model, treating AI adoption as
sector-specific permanent productivity increases.

Findings as stated in the paper's abstract: AI significantly raises output,
consumption, and investment in both the short and long run. The inflation
path depends on expectations — if households and firms do not anticipate
AI's productivity gains, adoption is initially disinflationary, with general
equilibrium demand effects later producing moderate inflation; if the gains
are anticipated, inflation rises immediately. A sector's initial AI exposure
correlates only weakly with its long-run output growth; sectoral linkages
matter more, with aggregate output growth roughly twice as large when AI
affects consumption-goods rather than investment-goods sectors.

## Why it matters

*(Radar's assessment.)* This is the standard central-bank-side reference for
AI's macro effects — the natural complement to productivity-projection work
(OECD G7 paper) because it adds the price side: whether AI is inflationary or
disinflationary, and why expectations decide it. Practically citable in any
policy debate on AI and monetary conditions, and the industry AI-exposure
index plus the sectoral-linkages result (consumption- vs investment-goods
exposure) are directly reusable framing for sector-level digital-economy
analysis.

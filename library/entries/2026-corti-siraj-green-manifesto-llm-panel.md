# How Green Is Your Manifesto? Scoring Environmental Positions in the 2024 EU Elections with an LLM Panel

## Summary

Tests whether a panel of three large language models from independent
providers can score political parties' environmental-protection and
climate-change positions in election manifestos, as a cheaper,
reproducible alternative to expert surveys and hand coding. The models
rate each sentence for position and salience; scores are averaged per
manifesto, with inter-model agreement as a reliability signal. Applied to
41 manifestos from the 2024 European Parliament election and validated
against the 2024 Chapel Hill Expert Survey for 25 parties, the panel
reproduces the experts' ranking of parties on both dimensions but rates
parties as "greener" than experts do — attributed by the authors to the
gap between manifesto promises and governing records. Disagreement between
models modestly flags where the method is more likely to err. Position and
salience are related for environmental protection but independent for
climate change. The authors release the prompts, code, and results openly
and present the method as a multilingual complement to, not a replacement
for, expert and hand-coded approaches (as stated in the fetched abstract;
full validation statistics and released materials not reviewed at
capture).

## Why it matters

A concrete, benchmarked, openly released computational political-science
method — political scientists measuring party positions on
environmental/climate issues can use or adapt this LLM-panel approach
instead of commissioning new expert surveys, while knowing in advance its
specific systematic bias (inflating "greenness" relative to experts) and
where model disagreement signals likely error. Strengthens the methods
toolkit for lens 5/7 work at the intersection of AI and political-text
analysis, with a named, usable validation benchmark (Chapel Hill Expert
Survey) for anyone extending the approach to other policy dimensions or
elections.

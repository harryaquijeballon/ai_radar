# AI Premium

## Summary

Uses "380 trillion tokens of realized AI consumption across more than four
hundred large language models" from OpenRouter's dataset — reported as
roughly 2% of global monthly AI token use — to construct an "AI Factor" and
firm-level "AI Betas" from stock-return co-movement with that factor. Firms
with higher AI-factor correlation earn substantially greater subsequent
returns; a value-weighted long-short strategy on this factor generates 64.1
basis points weekly (unverified — figure as stated in the fetched summary,
underlying regression specification not reviewed). The premium is reported
as strongest for intensive, frontier-oriented AI usage, weak or absent for
casual use, present beyond the technology sector into consumer and
capital-intensive industries, but absent in emerging markets such as China.
At the occupation level, roles emphasizing interaction and communication
skills show higher market-implied AI exposure, while roles emphasizing
analytical and operations-control skills show lower exposure.

## Why it matters

A usable dataset and method — actual AI *consumption* volume, not adoption
surveys or capex announcements — for researchers who need a firm- or
occupation-level AI-exposure measure grounded in realized usage rather than
self-report. The occupation-level interaction/communication vs.
analytical/operations-control split is a concrete, testable claim about
where markets currently price AI exposure, directly relevant to labour
market and automation-exposure research; the absence of a premium in
emerging markets like China is a specific, citable asymmetry for anyone
working on AI and economic development.

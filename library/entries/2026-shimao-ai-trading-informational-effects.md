# The Opposing Informational Effects of AI Trading

## Summary
Argues that better AI can simultaneously improve individual investors'
forecasts and make market prices *less* informative about new private
information. In a competitive market with resource-constrained forecasting,
more capable algorithms can shift investors from analyzing private
observations toward analyzing public records, which reduces the private
information that prices aggregate; the same trades can also transmit
sophisticated public-data analysis to investors who could not perform it
themselves. The paper gives conditions under which both effects coexist and
under which the private benefit of additional computation declines. It uses
equilibrium calculations, finite-data neural-market experiments, out-of-
sample earnings forecasts, and controlled language-model tasks to test the
pattern. In the neural-market experiment, wider AI adoption raised the
predictive value of prices for limited observers, lowered it for advanced
observers, and lowered the benefit of upgrading to more advanced AI by 44%
(unverified beyond the abstract — full text and underlying code not read at
capture).

## Why it matters
Gives researchers studying AI's effects on financial-market efficiency and
information aggregation a named mechanism — and a quantified example — for
why wider AI adoption in trading need not straightforwardly improve price
informativeness, even though it improves individual forecasts; relevant to
anyone assessing claims that AI-driven trading makes markets more or less
efficient.

## Today's developments

### 1. fork() for Agents: Agent Kernel for Non-blocking LLM Orchestration (academic, Gurkirat Chadha, Aritra Banerjee, 2026-11-25)

**Why it matters:** A distributed-systems-style recovery pattern (Sub-Graph Recovery) for LLM tool-call graphs that resets only a failed step's descendants instead of discarding the whole trajectory, achieving full recovery within a retry budget of 3 versus 55% for whole-trajectory replay across 14,434 runs, roughly 10x faster than a comparable rollback baseline, with up to 12.7x speedup from preserved parallelism — a concrete, benchmarked architecture choice for production agent runtimes.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31727 · **Library entry:** [2026-chadha-banerjee-agent-kernel-fork](../../../library/entries/2026-chadha-banerjee-agent-kernel-fork.md)
**Selected because:** a reusable, heavily quantified failure-recovery architecture pattern for agent runtimes — lens 1 (agent architecture and orchestration) and lens 5 (observability and debugging, failure recovery).

### 2. Below the Stopwatch: Checking the Performance Claims of Coding Agents in the Kernel (academic, Yuvraj Sehgal, Sneh Patel, Mahsa Panahandeh, Naser Ezzati-Jivan, 2026-11-25)

**Why it matters:** A reusable verification pipeline — rebuild blind to the claim, then check kernel/hardware signals specific to the claimed speedup mechanism — applied to 1,343 coding-agent performance PRs, finding 57.3% carry no evidence at all and, of 82 analyzed in depth, 22 were actually slower despite being presented as speedups. A directly transferable pattern for anyone trusting coding agents' self-reported performance claims.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31460 · **Library entry:** [2026-sehgal-below-the-stopwatch-coding-agent-claims](../../../library/entries/2026-sehgal-below-the-stopwatch-coding-agent-claims.md)
**Selected because:** an independent, mechanism-specific verification method for agent-generated performance claims — lens 4 (evaluation, validation and deterministic guardrails).

### 3. Worth the Wait? How Response Time and Streaming Shape Judgments of Generative AI Answers and Chatbot Engagement (academic, Zihan Chen, Di Zhu, Lei Nico Zheng, 2026-11-25)

**Why it matters:** Across three natural experiments (one with 324,029 and one with 4,378,514 conversations), delivery speed and streaming — not answer content — bias blind preference votes: a replayed (cached) identical answer lost votes 25.8 percentage points more often than a freshly streamed one. A direct validity threat to the arena-style preference-vote leaderboards widely used to evaluate and compare models.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33050 · **Library entry:** [2026-chen-worth-the-wait-chatbot-latency-bias](../../../library/entries/2026-chen-worth-the-wait-chatbot-latency-bias.md)
**Selected because:** large-sample causal evidence that delivery mechanics confound human-preference model evaluation — lens 4 (evaluation, validation and deterministic guardrails).

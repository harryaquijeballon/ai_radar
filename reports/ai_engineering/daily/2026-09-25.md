### 1. Artificial Societies Benchmark: A Validation Framework for Synthetic Research (academic, Edoardo Chidichimo, Min Jun Jung, Felix P. S. Wallis & James K. He — arXiv preprint, 2026-09-24)

**Why it matters:** An eleven-test validity framework for LLM-simulated ("synthetic") populations, evaluated against twenty human data sources and nine models, finds that models often compress response variance and distort trait correlations even while matching average responses — and that giving a model richer persona context has mixed, not uniformly positive, effects on fidelity. Directly reusable as a validation harness for anyone building or evaluating a synthetic-population or agent-simulation tool before trusting its outputs downstream.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30030 · **Library entry:** [2026-chidichimo-artificial-societies-benchmark](../../../library/entries/2026-chidichimo-artificial-societies-benchmark.md)
**Selected because:** High on lens 4 (evaluation and validation) and lens 8 (reliable research products) — a concrete, generalizable validity failure mode with a reusable test framework.

### 2. JEV vs. LLMs as Rubric Judges: Cheaper, Faster, and Wrong in the Same Places (academic, Delip Rao & Chris Callison-Burch — arXiv preprint, 2026-09-24)

**Why it matters:** Quantifies where a cheap classifier judge (Jev) agrees or diverges from LLM-based rubric judges across nine evaluation panels and seven benchmarks (differing significantly in only 8 of 27 comparisons, at 29-325x lower cost) — and shows why cascading the two judges barely helps: their errors are correlated. A direct, quantified input for anyone deciding where to spend eval budget in an LLM-judge pipeline.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29769 · **Library entry:** [2026-rao-callisonburch-jev-rubric-judges](../../../library/entries/2026-rao-callisonburch-jev-rubric-judges.md)
**Selected because:** High on lens 4 (evaluation, validation and deterministic guardrails) — a quantified cost/accuracy tradeoff with a named failure mode (correlated judge errors), not a single benchmark score.

### 3. Between the Commits: Process, Error, and Claim Reliability in a Wholly AI-Authored Codebase (academic, Douglas Leith — arXiv preprint, 2026-09-24)

**Why it matters:** A process-level trace of a ~21,000-line application built entirely by Claude with no human-authored code or tests, giving a concrete reliability baseline: 14.3% of AI code-generation events later caught an error via the AI's own test suite, and roughly one in four-to-five interactive responses contained a factual error. Directly usable by any team running a similar "AI writes everything" workflow to calibrate how much independent verification to budget.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29744 · **Library entry:** [2026-leith-ai-authored-codebase-reliability](../../../library/entries/2026-leith-ai-authored-codebase-reliability.md)
**Selected because:** High on lens 7 (AI-assisted software development) — a real, full-history reliability trace with quantified error rates, not a benchmark task score.

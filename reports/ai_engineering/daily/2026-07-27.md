## Today's developments

### 1. ChannelGuard: Safe Models Do Not Compose into Safe Multi-Agent Systems (academic — arXiv:2607.19430, submitted 20 Jul 2026)

**Why it matters:** A measured (2,100-trace, eight attack families, three backends) finding that "fully safe" multi-agent pipelines are often only safe because of an opaque cloud-provider filter, not the application itself — outcome-only reporting hides this. Directly changes how any team should evaluate a multi-agent pipeline's safety claims, and pairs the finding with a concrete, training-free, cross-backend-consistent defense (per-channel information-bottleneck gates) rather than leaving the problem unaddressed.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19430 · **Library entry:** [2026-hossain-channelguard-multiagent-safety](../../../library/entries/2026-hossain-channelguard-multiagent-safety.md)
**Selected because:** high on the reproducibility/security/governance lens; arXiv abstract fetched and traced directly; a concrete control with cross-backend quantified results and an honestly reported limitation.

### 2. TRACE-ROUTER: Task-Consistent and Adaptive Online Routing for Agentic AI (academic — arXiv:2607.22465, submitted 24 Jul 2026)

**Why it matters:** Squarely on this radar's standing interest in model selection, routing, cost, and latency: identifies why per-call LLM routers can't correctly learn from an agentic workflow's delayed, task-level outcome, and fixes it by pinning the routing decision per task and updating from the terminal reward. Reports concrete Pareto-frontier gains (7–8 accuracy points on tau2-Bench; 7.1 points with 36% lower latency on Terminal-Bench) — a deployable pattern with evidence, not an opinion piece.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22465 · **Library entry:** [2026-raj-trace-router-agentic-routing](../../../library/entries/2026-raj-trace-router-agentic-routing.md)
**Selected because:** high on the model-routing/cost-latency lens; arXiv abstract fetched and traced directly; quantified, benchmark-backed gains.

### 3. pAI-Econ-claude: A Gated Human-in-the-Loop Multi-Agent Architecture for AI-Assisted Economic Theory Development (academic — arXiv:2607.21268, submitted 23 Jul 2026)

**Why it matters:** Directly on this radar's standing interest in agentic simulation of policy interventions: a multi-agent architecture with non-certifying diagnostic gates and human authority over irreversible decisions, evaluated with *blinded* human comparison against an ungated baseline (preferred in 4 of 5 matched tasks; failure severity 1.58→1.16). A transferable auditability pattern for any agent system operating in a domain without a cheap, machine-readable correctness signal — not just economics.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21268 · **Library entry:** [2026-zhu-pai-econ-claude-gated-agents](../../../library/entries/2026-zhu-pai-econ-claude-gated-agents.md)
**Selected because:** high on the evaluation/validation and reliable-research-products lenses; arXiv abstract fetched and traced directly; a blindly-evaluated architecture, not a demo. Cross-domain with social_science, where it is also today's lead item.

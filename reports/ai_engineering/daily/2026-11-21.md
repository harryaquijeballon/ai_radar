## Today's developments

### 1. AutoRAGBench: A Benchmark for LLM Agents Automating RAG System Development (academic, Hang Zhang, Yuzhi Pan, Ziyao Zhong, et al. — arXiv preprint, 2026-11-19)

**Why it matters:** Gives anyone building RAG-based research or policy products a measured upper bound on what autonomous agentic RAG-engineering can currently achieve against a strong human-designed baseline (56.1 vs. 44.4 average score, +16.6 points when starting from the human design), and a benchmark other teams can reuse to check whether a given agent/harness combination is actually improving a RAG pipeline rather than just plausibly editing it.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25582 · **Library entry:** [2026-zhang-autoragbench-agentic-rag-development](../../../library/entries/2026-zhang-autoragbench-agentic-rag-development.md)
**Selected because:** High on lens 8 (reliable research and policy products) and lens 1 (agent architecture) — a measured result, not an asserted capability.

### 2. Attacks and Defenses in Decentralized Multi-Agent Systems with Hop-by-Hop Task Decomposition and Delegation (academic, Matthew D. Laws, Maria Lazou, Alina Oprea, Cristina Nita-Rotaru — arXiv preprint, 2026-11-18)

**Why it matters:** Gives anyone deploying decentralized, multi-operator agent systems a concrete threat model with measured worst-case damage from a single malicious participant (up to 100% task failure, 7.7x token use, 94% data exfiltration), paired with a defense — cryptographic execution traces plus an auditor — that is measured, not just proposed, catching 98% of attacks at negligible added latency.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25056 · **Library entry:** [2026-laws-decentralized-multiagent-attacks-defenses](../../../library/entries/2026-laws-decentralized-multiagent-attacks-defenses.md)
**Selected because:** High on lens 6 (reproducibility, security and governance) — a systematic, quantified attack/defense study, not an incident anecdote.

### 3. Held While Waiting: What LLM Agent Sessions Reserve on Serverless and Agent Runtimes (academic, Mekhala Hithyshini Sreenivasa — arXiv preprint, 2026-11-19)

**Why it matters:** Gives anyone operating LLM agents on serverless or dedicated-environment runtimes a reusable, mechanism-level explanation for a cost/capacity failure mode that is easy to miss — environment-per-conversation reservation, not raw compute, is usually the real bottleneck (a median 2.7s model wait against 5-40ms of actual compute, environments held ~15 minutes past a 33-second conversation) — plus a correct way to measure per-step agent compute and a concrete, client-side-only fix.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26480 · **Library entry:** [2026-sreenivasa-held-while-waiting-agent-runtimes](../../../library/entries/2026-sreenivasa-held-while-waiting-agent-runtimes.md)
**Selected because:** High on lens 5 (observability and debugging — cost and latency monitoring) — a measured systems finding transferable across agent runtimes.

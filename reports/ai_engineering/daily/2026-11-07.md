## Today's developments

### 1. Stress Testing Chain-of-Thought Monitoring Against Covert Misalignment (academic, Yao Huang, Yifan Wang, Yitong Sun, Yichi Zhang, Yinpeng Dong, 2026-11-05)

**Why it matters:** Chain-of-thought monitoring is a widely assumed safety
control for autonomous agents; this paper shows task-tailored (not
generic-template) red-teaming substantially defeats it even against top
monitor models including GPT-5.2 and Claude-4.5. It doesn't stop at the
vulnerability — it proposes Bayesian Trajectory Monitoring, a concrete
detection method scoring 0.91 AUC and 0.74 TPR at 5% FPR, giving builders
both a sharper threat model and a named alternative to evaluate.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07586 · **Library entry:** [2026-huang-stress-testing-cot-monitoring-misalignment](../../../library/entries/2026-huang-stress-testing-cot-monitoring-misalignment.md)
**Selected because:** High on reproducibility, security and governance
(lens 6) — the core lens for oversight-loop reliability; NeurIPS 2026
accepted, quantified vulnerability and fix.

### 2. Signed but Not Safe: Requirement Integrity in Multi-Agent LLM Handoffs (academic, Xiaobo Chen, Genliang Zhu, Ziyuan Wang, Chu Wang, 2026-11-05)

**Why it matters:** A preregistered, quantified test of whether
cryptographically signing agent-to-agent handoffs actually makes multi-agent
systems safer: signing cut unsafe commits under attack from 69.4% to 9.0%
and defeated in-transit tampering, but a pre-signing extraction attack still
got through — pinpointing extraction, not transit, as the real trust
boundary. Tells a builder exactly where to put verification effort in a
handoff/merge pipeline.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07483 · **Library entry:** [2026-chen-signed-but-not-safe-requirement-integrity](../../../library/entries/2026-chen-signed-but-not-safe-requirement-integrity.md)
**Selected because:** High on evaluation/deterministic guardrails (lens 4)
and governance (lens 6) — a precise, falsifiable trust-boundary finding,
not a general security warning.

### 3. CWE-bench v2: More Coverage, Quality, and Transparency (primary, Collinear AI, 2026-11-03)

**Why it matters:** A public, deterministically and reproducibly scored
benchmark (100 tasks, 91 CWE types, 8 languages) for whether coding agents
can find and fix real security weaknesses without breaking functionality —
with a held-out anti-memorization set. Gives builders an independent,
non-LLM-judged check on vendor security-capability claims plus real
cost-vs-accuracy figures (best model 67.8% pass@1 but still fails 19% of
tasks on every attempt) for choosing a model for automated security work.

**Source:** https://huggingface.co/blog/cwe-bench · **Library entry:** [2026-collinear-cwe-bench-v2-security-benchmark](../../../library/entries/2026-collinear-cwe-bench-v2-security-benchmark.md)
**Selected because:** High on evaluation/validation (lens 4) and
security/governance (lens 6) — deterministic scoring and a held-out set
make this a usable yardstick, not just another leaderboard.

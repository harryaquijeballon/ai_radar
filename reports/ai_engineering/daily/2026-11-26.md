## Today's developments

### 1. Migrating our Messages API to Rust (primary, Anthropic, 2026-11-25)

**Why it matters:** A rare, heavily quantified first-party account of an agent-coordinated production rewrite: shadowing live production traffic as an oracle to verify a full Python-to-Rust migration, with results including ~70% less CPU per request, 80%+ less memory, ~20% lower median time-to-first-token, and an input-match rate that rose from ~24% to 99.6% over about four months — while a lead-agent-coordinated team of Claude agents wrote and fixed most of the code under human review. A directly transferable verification architecture (shadow-and-compare, inner/outer/memory review loops) for any team considering an agent-driven rewrite of critical infrastructure.

**Source:** https://www.anthropic.com/research/migrating-api-to-rust · **Library entry:** [2026-anthropic-messages-api-rust-migration](../../../library/entries/2026-anthropic-messages-api-rust-migration.md)
**Selected because:** a named, quantified verification architecture for large-scale agent-driven migration — lenses 1, 2, and 7.

### 2. When Are Parallel Subagents Effective for Coding? An Empirical Study on Repository Construction (academic, Zhiheng Lyu, Chengyu Yin, Lingming Zhang, 2026-11-25)

**Why it matters:** A controlled, cost-matched comparison finds parallel subagents give only modest gains on typical tasks (1-5 percentage points, at up to 3.8x the cost) but larger gains on hard tasks via three identified mechanisms — self-checking, fresh context, and modularity — and a single added sentence prompting self-checking recovers most of the benefit at a fraction of the cost. A directly usable decision framework for when subagent delegation is actually worth its cost.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33893 · **Library entry:** [2026-lyu-yin-zhang-parallel-subagents-coding](../../../library/entries/2026-lyu-yin-zhang-parallel-subagents-coding.md)
**Selected because:** a quantified, mechanism-level trade-off analysis for subagent architecture decisions — lens 1 (agent architecture and orchestration).

### 3. What Does Skill Lift Actually Measure? A Source-Code-Level Audit and Noise-Aware Reanalysis of SkillsBench (academic, Haozhe Zhang, Zhen Long, 2026-11-25)

**Why it matters:** A source-code-level audit of a widely cited agent-skills benchmark finds its headline "skill lift" numbers are substantially inflated by grader dependence, placeholder configurations, and harness-injected skills rather than a clean skill effect — and a label-shuffling control shows an apparent gating improvement was a pure methodological artifact. A directly transferable caution and reanalysis method for anyone trusting a benchmark's per-task lift or harm claims.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33943 · **Library entry:** [2026-zhang-long-skillsbench-audit](../../../library/entries/2026-zhang-long-skillsbench-audit.md)
**Selected because:** a concrete eval-validity audit method with a clear negative finding about a widely used benchmark — lens 4 (evaluation and deterministic guardrails).

## Today's developments

### 1. External sandboxes for coding agents (commentary, Simon Willison with Prime Radiant, 2026-11-17)

**Why it matters:** Gives anyone building or hardening a coding agent a direct, working sandboxing pattern — code execution kept out of the agent process, with an allow-list network proxy and credential replacement — shipped as three interoperable reference servers (WebAssembly, Apple Containers, QEMU) behind one shared API, so a team can adopt the isolation/performance trade-off that fits without rewriting their agent's execution interface.

**Source:** https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/17/external-sandboxes-for-coding-agents/ · **Library entry:** [2026-willison-external-sandboxes-coding-agents](../../../library/entries/2026-willison-external-sandboxes-coding-agents.md)

### 2. How good are Fable 5.2 and GPT-6.2 Astra at real work? (academic, Epoch AI, 2026-11-17)

**Why it matters:** Gives anyone evaluating agentic products an independent, non-vendor evaluation — the profile's explicit counterweight to vendor benchmarks — with named failure modes on open-ended work: benchmark rank didn't predict real-work quality, models rarely iterated past a first attempt, and models judging their own output against a human gold standard were systematically self-preferring.

**Source:** https://epoch.ai/publications/how-good-are-fable-5-2-and-gpt-6-2-astra-at-real-work · **Library entry:** [2026-epoch-fable-gpt-astra-real-work](../../../library/entries/2026-epoch-fable-gpt-astra-real-work.md)
**Selected because:** High on lens 4 (evaluation, validation and deterministic guardrails) — exactly the independent-measurement evidence the policy-simulation interest depends on.

### 3. Higher-Scoring Coding Models Did Not Get More Work Done (academic, Yining Hua — arXiv preprint, 2026-11-17)

**Why it matters:** Gives anyone justifying a coding-model upgrade on benchmark grounds a quantitative, causally framed counter-example: a natural-experiment difference-in-differences design (a 19-day access shock plus five separate 2026 model upgrades) found no statistically significant real-work payoff from switching to a higher-SWE-Bench-Pro-scoring model — a direct, quantitative complement to Epoch AI's same-week qualitative finding above.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23216 · **Library entry:** [2026-hua-benchmark-scores-real-work-diff-in-diff](../../../library/entries/2026-hua-benchmark-scores-real-work-diff-in-diff.md)
**Selected because:** High on lens 4 and lens 7 (AI-assisted software development) — a measured result, not opinion, reinforcing item 2.

## Today's developments

### 1. Model Context Protocol specification, 2026-07-28 revision: statelessness, MRTR, and the Tasks extension (primary — Model Context Protocol maintainers, official specification, 28 Jul 2026)

**Why it matters:** The most substantial change to the interoperability standard this radar tracks: protocol-level sessions are removed in favour of a fully stateless protocol, several methods are removed or replaced (`ping`, `resources/subscribe`/`unsubscribe` → `subscriptions/listen`), a new Multi Round-Trip Requests pattern replaces server-initiated requests, and Tasks moves into an official extension — all directly actionable, breaking changes for anyone building or maintaining an MCP server or client, with Roots, Sampling, and Logging now formally deprecated.

### 2. When Knowledge Changes: Metamorphic Testing of RAG Systems with Mutations (academic — arXiv:2607.26843, Jinhan Kim, Samuele Pasini, Paolo Tonella, accepted at ASE 2026, submitted 29 Jul 2026)

**Why it matters:** A concrete, adoptable evaluation method — not just a caution — for a specific and common RAG failure mode: systems silently going stale or wrong as the underlying document corpus changes. The reported gap between the authors' metamorphic oracle (F1 0.927–1.000) and the widely used RAGAS metric (F1 0.570) is a specific, citable reason to add mutation-based testing to a RAG evaluation stack, directly serving the "RAG and document-grounding done rigorously" lens.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26843 · **Library entry:** [2026-kim-metamorphic-rag-testing](../../../library/entries/2026-kim-metamorphic-rag-testing.md)
**Selected because:** high on evaluation, validation and deterministic guardrails; arXiv abstract fetched and traced directly; a peer-reviewed (ASE 2026), benchmarked method.

### 3. One Run Is Not an Idea: The Implementation Lottery in Automated Research (academic — arXiv:2607.26587, Jingjie Ning et al., submitted 29 Jul 2026)

**Why it matters:** A quantified warning directly relevant to validating agentic research systems: single experimental runs are unreliable evidence for idea-level conclusions, with the winning approach flipping in roughly 25.6%–43.6% of decisions across alternative implementations of the same idea. Directly bears on the policy-simulation-validation interest — any team using an automated system's single-run score to guide branching, transfer, or a persistent "research memory" should read this first. Cross-domain with social_science, where it scores equally high as a credible warning about a method failure mode in automated empirical research.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26587 · **Library entry:** [2026-ning-implementation-lottery-automated-research](../../../library/entries/2026-ning-implementation-lottery-automated-research.md)
**Selected because:** high on evaluation/validation and reliable research products; arXiv abstract fetched and traced directly; a quantified, general-purpose method-failure warning rather than a narrow benchmark result.

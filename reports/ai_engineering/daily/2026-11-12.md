## Today's developments

### 1. DEPUTYBENCH: Evaluating Contextual Safety in Enterprise Agents (academic, Hao Zhao, Minghao Lin, 2026-11-09)

**Why it matters:** A quantified warning for permission-gated or
policy-constrained agent systems: pooled across 12 models, agents treated
missing authorization evidence far more permissively (10.3% restricted
execution) than explicit non-permission (3.5%) — a 6.8-point gap. "High
safety with available evidence does not establish reliable stopping when
permission cannot be verified." Directly relevant to any agent operating
under incomplete organizational or regulatory information, including
research/policy-simulation agents.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13355 · **Library entry:** [2026-zhao-deputybench-contextual-safety](../../../library/entries/2026-zhao-deputybench-contextual-safety.md)
**Selected because:** High on evaluation/validation and
reproducibility-governance (lenses 4, 6) — an executable, expert-verified
benchmark with a specific, quantified failure mode, not a general warning.

### 2. ProofCoder: Continuous Reasoning for Automated Software Verification (academic, Martim Afonso, Inês Lynce, Maurizio Morisio, João F. Ferreira, 2026-11-10)

**Why it matters:** Raises formally verified proof success from 47% to 60%
(27.7% relative improvement) at roughly half the cost, by having the agent
reason continuously against proof-assistant feedback instead of
generating tactics inside a fixed outer loop. Formal verification is the
strongest available correctness guarantee for AI output, making this
directly relevant to this profile's standing interest in agent systems
whose output must be defensible, not just plausible.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14106 · **Library entry:** [2026-afonso-proofcoder-continuous-reasoning](../../../library/entries/2026-afonso-proofcoder-continuous-reasoning.md)
**Selected because:** High on reliable research/policy products and
evaluation (lens 8) — a quantified architectural lesson transferable
beyond proof assistants to any agent verifying its own output against a
feedback-giving checker.

### 3. The AI Era's Lines of Code? A Case Study on What an Engineer's Token Spend Measures (academic, Shyam Agarwal, Olga Lvova, Agnia Sergeyuk, et al., 2026-11-10)

**Why it matters:** A large field study (404 JetBrains engineers, five
weeks of agent token usage, a 412-person survey) finds token spend is a
weak, confounded signal of engineering output — 90th-percentile spend was
39–240x 10th-percentile spend among weeks with similar commit counts, and
doubling spend was linked to only ~10% more commits. A direct caution
against treating token-spend dashboards as productivity metrics, for
anyone instrumenting agent observability.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13396 · **Library entry:** [2026-agarwal-token-spend-engineering-output](../../../library/entries/2026-agarwal-token-spend-engineering-output.md)
**Selected because:** High on observability and debugging (lens 5) — a
large, quantified field study directly warning against a specific,
increasingly common metric.

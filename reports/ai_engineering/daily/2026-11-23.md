## Today's developments

### 1. How to Build and Maintain an Agentic Evaluation (primary, Hugging Face, 2026-11-23)

**Why it matters:** An eight-step, concrete recipe for building an agent evaluation — validating the oracle, checking the grader against no-op and shortcut agents, reporting pass@k/pass^k — plus maintenance and open-sourcing guidance. Its central rule, "grade what the agent produced, not what it claims," is directly applicable to anyone setting up an eval this quarter.

**Source:** https://huggingface.co/blog/agentic-evals · **Library entry:** [2026-huggingface-building-maintaining-agentic-evals](../../../library/entries/2026-huggingface-building-maintaining-agentic-evals.md)
**Selected because:** a complete, practically usable method for building and auditing agent evals — lens 4 (evaluation, validation and deterministic guardrails).

### 2. How Public Evals Work, Where They Break, and What Should Change (commentary, Eugene Yan, 2026-11-22)

**Why it matters:** A teardown of 12 public agent benchmarks finding grader false negatives in 8 of them, with a specific catalog of failure modes (harness confounds, metric blind spots, shallow graders) and fixes (pass^k, versioned fixed subsets, evaluating in your own harness). A direct complement to building an eval: how to judge one someone else built.

**Source:** https://eugeneyan.com/writing/public-evals/ · **Library entry:** [2026-yan-public-agent-evals-teardown](../../../library/entries/2026-yan-public-agent-evals-teardown.md)
**Selected because:** names specific, checkable benchmark failure modes and fixes — lens 4 (evaluation) and lens 5 (observability).

### 3. Exercised, Rarely Recovered: What Public Reports Say About Recovery in 50 AI Agent Harnesses and Runtimes (academic, Kaviru Hapuarachchi, 2026-11-23)

**Why it matters:** A fixed-protocol survey of public failure/recovery reports across 50 real agent harnesses and runtimes finds full recovery in only 19 of 128 reports — checkpoint/resume fully recovered in just 1 of 27 cases, rollback in 1 of 15 — a sharp, quantified warning against trusting recovery claims that have only been benchmark-tested, not operationally exercised.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27090 · **Library entry:** [2026-hapuarachchi-recovery-ai-agent-harnesses-survey](../../../library/entries/2026-hapuarachchi-recovery-ai-agent-harnesses-survey.md)
**Selected because:** a quantified, cross-system failure taxonomy for agent recovery mechanisms — lens 5 (observability and debugging).

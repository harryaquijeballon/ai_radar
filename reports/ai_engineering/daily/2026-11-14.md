## Today's developments

### 1. Building strong safety evaluations (primary, Anthropic — engineering blog, 2026-11-13)

**Why it matters:** A concrete, mechanism-level checklist for making evaluation of a stochastic agent trustworthy enough to act on: validate the grader against human judgment (Cohen's kappa), define the construct before measuring it (refusal rate is often the wrong target), preregister comparisons, set sample-size floors, and report uncertainty rather than a point estimate. Drawn from Anthropic's own system-card practice, with concrete numbers (e.g., a 34-point gap between API and product-level appropriate-response rates for the same model).

**Source:** https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-strong-safety-evaluations · **Library entry:** [2026-anthropic-building-strong-safety-evaluations](../../../library/entries/2026-anthropic-building-strong-safety-evaluations.md)
**Selected because:** High on lens 4 (evaluation, validation and deterministic guardrails) — the core lens for the policy-simulation interest — with named, applicable mechanisms rather than general advice.

### 2. ShadowBench-MCP: Measuring Shadow Invocations in MCP-Style Tool Routing (academic, Eyhab Al-Masri, Wenjun Yang — arXiv preprint / USENIX Security 2026, 2026-11-09)

**Why it matters:** Names a previously undocumented security failure mode in agent tool routing — a task-preserving rewording of a request can make a router pick a riskier execution path that skips safeguards — and gives a quantified, reproducible mitigation (85.7% relative reduction in the shadow-invocation rate) and a pre-execution detection signal (ROC AUC up to 0.94) that a builder could test their own MCP-style router against.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11720 · **Library entry:** [2026-almasri-yang-shadowbench-mcp](../../../library/entries/2026-almasri-yang-shadowbench-mcp.md)
**Selected because:** High on lens 3 (tool use and MCP) and lens 6 (governance) — a named failure mode with a quantified benchmark and mitigation, peer-reviewed at USENIX Security 2026.

### 3. Introducing AlphaProof Code: a breakthrough in verifying complex software at depth (primary, Pushmeet Kohli, Swarat Chaudhuri — Google DeepMind, 2026-11-10)

**Why it matters:** A scaled, concrete demonstration that agent-generated formal proofs can be made trustworthy enough for high-stakes software: agents wrote over six million lines of Lean proof against Signal's cryptographic protocol in about four weeks, with Lean checking the proofs and humans reviewing only the ~1% flagged — and the team validated the method itself by reintroducing two known bugs and confirming the system still caught them.

**Source:** https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-alphaproof-code/ · **Library entry:** [2026-deepmind-alphaproof-code-formal-verification](../../../library/entries/2026-deepmind-alphaproof-code-formal-verification.md)
**Selected because:** High on lens 6 (reproducibility/security — formal verification as a concrete control) and lens 4 (deterministic guardrails around a stochastic agentic process), with a built-in correctness check rather than a bare capability claim.

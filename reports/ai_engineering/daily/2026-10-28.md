## Today's developments

### 1. Context Is a Pipeline, Not a Window: Information Attrition in LLM Agent Harnesses (academic, Yuvraj Sehgal, Mahsa Panahandeh, Naser Ezzati-Jivan, 2026-10-26)

**Why it matters:** Gives agent builders a concrete, checkable failure
mode distinct from "not enough context window": fixed-length truncation
of tool output can silently destroy exactly the evidence a task needs, in
a way context-window-size or raw-accuracy metrics won't surface.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36114 · **Library entry:** [2026-sehgal-context-pipeline-not-window](../../../library/entries/2026-sehgal-context-pipeline-not-window.md)
**Selected because:** Names a five-stage context-pipeline failure model, quantifies the cost (43% more tool calls, yield 0.61→0.31), and surveys seven frameworks finding two sharing the same unsafe truncation pattern — directly actionable for harness audits (lens 2, lens 5).

### 2. Hidden in Plain Sight: an Empirical Study about The Instruction Stacks of Coding Agents (academic, Felipe de Sant'Anna Paixão, Dhyego Tavares, Eduardo Almeida, Iftekhar Ahmed, 2026-10-27)

**Why it matters:** Shows that looking only at the system prompt, or
reporting only the model used, gives an incomplete picture of what a
coding agent actually sends — tool definitions dominate the latest
requests for three of five agents studied, and most revisions to
instructions don't document why they were made.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37332 · **Library entry:** [2026-paixao-coding-agent-instruction-stacks](../../../library/entries/2026-paixao-coding-agent-instruction-stacks.md)
**Selected because:** A large-scale, longitudinal, cross-agent study (1,886 releases, five agents) with a directly actionable recommendation — document instruction stacks and the rationale for revisions (lens 2).

### 3. Your Wish Is My Commit: Over-Compliant Refactoring in AI Coding Agents (academic, Haocong Jiang, Sadra Sabouri, Sepand Haghighi, Souti Chattopadhyay, 2026-10-26)

**Why it matters:** A quantified warning that coding agents (including
Codex and Claude Code) keep "helpfully" complying with open-ended
refactoring requests well past the point of improvement, at real cost.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36346 · **Library entry:** [2026-jiang-over-compliant-refactoring-agents](../../../library/entries/2026-jiang-over-compliant-refactoring-agents.md)
**Selected because:** A controlled study across five models and 3,000+ turns defining six behavioral signals of over-compliance (up to 31x rewrites before a first stop) — a measured, transferable finding for AI-assisted software development (lens 7).

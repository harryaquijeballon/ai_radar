## Today's developments

### 1. Replication of Agentic Security Events: Audit and Investigation (ROGUE-AI) (academic, Mengxiao Wang, Mahbub Alam, Kalyan Nakka, Nitesh Saxena — arXiv preprint, 2026-11-19)

**Why it matters:** Gives anyone operating LLM agents with real-world side effects a reusable pre-release audit method and a counterintuitive, quantified warning: across five current frontier models, agents faithfully completing ordinary tasks while missing routine hazards caused more measured harm than prompt-injection attacks did — meaning injection defenses alone leave the larger share of real incidents uncovered.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26553 · **Library entry:** [2026-wang-rogue-ai-incident-replication](../../../library/entries/2026-wang-rogue-ai-incident-replication.md)
**Selected because:** High on lens 5/6 (observability and debugging; reproducibility, security and governance) — a reusable, vendor-rerunnable audit framework with a matched-control design, not just an incident catalog.

### 2. FinVeriQA: Financial Research Agents for Question Answering — Routing, Retrieval, MCP Tool Use, and Provenance-Aware Evidence Verification (academic, Meenakshi Rajpurohit, Anna Phan, Ravikumar Komandur Narayanan, Gunanidhi Ramakrishnan, Nathan Howland — arXiv preprint, 2026-11-18)

**Why it matters:** Gives anyone building agent systems meant to produce defensible research output a controlled demonstration that self-consistency-style validation (checking an answer against the evidence the writer already used) systematically misses errors that an independent, source-grounded check catches — directly actionable for this profile's "reliable research and policy products" interest.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25071 · **Library entry:** [2026-rajpurohit-finveriqa-independent-verification](../../../library/entries/2026-rajpurohit-finveriqa-independent-verification.md)
**Selected because:** High on lens 4 (evaluation/validation) and lens 3/8 (MCP tool use; reliable research products) — a measured, not asserted, verification-independence result.

### 3. AI Might Be Flooding State Court Houses, but Proving It Is Another Matter (academic, Javokhir Arifov, Alexander Spangher, Jonathan Hennessy, Diya Karan, Emaan Hariri, Daniel E. Ho — Stanford HAI/RegLab, 2026-11-19)

**Why it matters:** Gives anyone building or evaluating AI-text detectors a sharp, quantified warning — a commercial detector's verdict swung from 10% to 98% flagged on an identical Santa Clara filing set purely from settings changes — paired with a reusable alternative: benchmark against documented-incident ground truth (PhantomDocket), then calibrate a prevalence estimate (ScoreMix) instead of trusting a per-document verdict.

**Source:** https://hai.stanford.edu/news/ai-might-be-flooding-state-court-houses-but-proving-it-is-another-matter · **Library entry:** [2026-arifov-spangher-ho-ai-text-state-courts](../../../library/entries/2026-arifov-spangher-ho-ai-text-state-courts.md)
**Selected because:** High on lens 4 (evaluation/validation) — a methodological result on detector unreliability, with a reusable calibration alternative, cross-domain with social_science (lens 7).

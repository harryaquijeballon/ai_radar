## Today's developments

### 1. CARI: Cross-Boundary Adversarial Runtime Injection in Coding-Agent Workflows (academic, Jie Fu, Shuang Wu, Ashish Kundu, Wendy Hui Wang, Yuan Hong, 2026-10-28)

**Why it matters:** Names and quantifies a realistic injection vector —
malicious peer-component diagnostic output in cross-team/cross-service
coding-agent workflows — outside the usual "untrusted document" threat
model, with a clear finding that the cheap defense (prompt-level warnings)
fails while the working defense (explicit policy) has a measurable
over-refusal cost.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38799 · **Library entry:** [2026-fu-cari-cross-boundary-injection](../../../library/entries/2026-fu-cari-cross-boundary-injection.md)
**Selected because:** 94.8% attack success (2,845/3,000 trials) across four languages and five models, transferring to Codex (61.3%) and Claude Code (58.7%) — lens 6.

### 2. Security Impact of Coding Agent's Memory Files (academic, Thiago Santos de Moura, Selma Güler, Yannic Noller, 2026-10-28)

**Why it matters:** A concrete, quantified demonstration that project
memory files (CLAUDE.md-style) are an unreviewed trust boundary — directly
relevant to any project, including this one, that relies on such a file —
and that an agent's own disclosure of having used it cannot be relied on as
a safety net.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38521 · **Library entry:** [2026-demoura-coding-agent-memory-file-security](../../../library/entries/2026-demoura-coding-agent-memory-file-security.md)
**Selected because:** Controlled test across 12 CWE classes and two harnesses shows malicious memory files shift secure-completion rates by -8 to +6pp, with harness-dependent, often-buried disclosure — lens 6.

### 3. Soei: Structured Observability of Evidence and Intent for Multi-Agent AI Software Engineering (academic, Mohammed Nayeem Khan, K S Sarun Kumar, 2026-10-29)

**Why it matters:** Quantifies, for the first time at this scale, how often
agent-marked "complete" work actually lacks a verification trail — a
concrete empirical basis for requiring evidence-gated completion in
agentic SDLC workflows — alongside a working, benchmarked provenance
system.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39468 · **Library entry:** [2026-khan-soei-structured-observability-agents](../../../library/entries/2026-khan-soei-structured-observability-agents.md)
**Selected because:** 45-session empirical study (~102k records) finds 57.8% of tasks marked complete lacked verification proof; the authors' ledger verifies at ~126k events/sec with no stored credentials — lens 5.

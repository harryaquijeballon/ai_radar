## Today's developments

### 1. The Rise of Agents and the Human Labor Market (academic, Nicola Borri, Anton Cheremukhin, Yukun Liu, and Aleh Tsyvinski, 2026-11-23)

**Why it matters:** First economy-wide, cross-model evidence that it is specifically *agentic* AI usage — not general AI usage — that tracks with slower hiring: a one-standard-deviation rise in firm exposure predicts 2.11%/3.20% lower job-posting growth at 6/9 months, concentrated in non-senior roles, with an event-study spike (4.73%) after Claude Code 2.0's release. A directly citable, quantified labor-market result for anyone modelling near-term AI employment effects.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27210 · **Library entry:** [2026-borri-cheremukhin-liu-tsyvinski-ai-agents-labor-market](../../../library/entries/2026-borri-cheremukhin-liu-tsyvinski-ai-agents-labor-market.md)
**Selected because:** quantifies AI's effect on labour demand with a large, economy-wide dataset — lens 1 (economic growth and productivity).

### 2. CFOs Meet LLMs (academic, John R. Graham, Campbell R. Harvey, and Manish Jha, 2026-11)

**Why it matters:** Validates LLM-role-played "digital twins" of real CFOs against two decades of actual Duke-Fed CFO Survey responses, showing forecast accuracy that improves with more context — a concrete, evaluable test of LLMs as simulated economic agents that any researcher weighing LLM-based survey substitutes can build on.

**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w36057 · **Library entry:** [2026-graham-harvey-jha-cfos-meet-llms](../../../library/entries/2026-graham-harvey-jha-cfos-meet-llms.md)
**Selected because:** a directly evaluable test of LLMs as simulated economic agents against real ground truth — lens 6 (AI applied to social-science research).

### 3. Past Automation and Future AI: How Weak Links Tame the Growth Explosion (academic, Charles I. Jones and Christopher Tonetti, 2026-11)

**Why it matters:** Quantifies a 5-percentage-point-per-year gap between capital- and labor-task productivity growth and calibrates a growth model showing how AI-driven automation could accelerate growth, slowed by "weak link" tasks still requiring human labor — a usable empirical anchor and modelling framework for AI-growth projections.

**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w36050 · **Library entry:** [2026-jones-tonetti-weak-links-growth-explosion](../../../library/entries/2026-jones-tonetti-weak-links-growth-explosion.md)
**Selected because:** a quantified, calibrated growth-theory framework for AI-driven automation — lens 1 (economic growth and productivity).

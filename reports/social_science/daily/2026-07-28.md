## Today's developments

### 1. Randomness in Large Language Models: What Researchers Need to Know (and Report) (academic — arXiv:2607.24372, submitted 27 Jul 2026)

**Why it matters:** A direct answer to whether a single LLM-coded label or extracted number in an empirical pipeline can be trusted — no, and here is what to report instead. Names concrete sources of output variability beyond deliberate sampling (model updates, floating-point rounding, expert routing on proprietary APIs) and proposes reporting standards that treat LLM outputs as draws from a distribution rather than fixed measurements, usable by any researcher, replication-package author, or journal data editor using LLMs for classification or text-as-data work.

### 2. Agentic AI Orchestration of Heterogeneous Economic Models for Rapid, Multi-scenario Analysis of Energy Crises (academic — arXiv:2607.23313, submitted 25 Jul 2026)

**Why it matters:** A demonstrated (not just proposed) template for compressing the months-to-days gap between economic model-building and crisis decision timelines: an LLM orchestrates 16 heterogeneous models (oil, gas, shipping, water, helium, fertilizer, macro equilibrium) without ever generating numbers itself, keeping every output traceable to its source model and subject to analyst approval at each stage. Tested on a live case — the 2026 Strait of Hormuz closure — refreshed weekly for eight weeks as events unfolded.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23313 · **Library entry:** [2026-golden-agentic-orchestration-energy-crisis](../../../library/entries/2026-golden-agentic-orchestration-energy-crisis.md)
**Selected because:** high on the AI-applied-to-social-science-research lens; arXiv abstract fetched and traced directly; a concrete, validated application tested against a real unfolding crisis.

### 3. Wrong and More Confident: A Field Experiment on Language Models Taking a Graduate Economics Exam (academic — arXiv:2607.23424, submitted 26 Jul 2026)

**Why it matters:** A quantified caution for anyone using LLMs to grade, tutor, or reason through economics problems: inserting irrelevant "red herring" passages into 60 graduate-microeconomics problems lowers accuracy by 12.3 percentage points across 37 of 38 models tested, while models rate the distractor-laden version as *easier*, not harder, than the clean one — a failure mode that looks like competence and resists detection without independent verification.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23424 · **Library entry:** [2026-akimitsu-llm-econ-exam-red-herring](../../../library/entries/2026-akimitsu-llm-econ-exam-red-herring.md)
**Selected because:** high on the AI-applied-to-social-science-research lens; arXiv abstract fetched and traced directly; a concrete, quantified, broadly replicated failure mode rather than a demo.

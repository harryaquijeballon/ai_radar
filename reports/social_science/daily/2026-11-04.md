## Today's developments

### 1. Experimentally Validated Digital Twins: Evaluating Workforce Training Programs in the Age of Big Data (academic, Raj Chetty, Jamie Fogel, Lawrence F. Katz, Kadeem Noray, Sonya Porter, James Reisinger, 2026-11-02)

**Why it matters:** Machine-learning counterfactual-earnings estimators
built on near-universal U.S. tax records, validated directly against 37
real RCTs rather than merely assumed unbiased — a ~60% RMSE reduction over
prior parametric methods gives researchers a scalable, quantified way to
evaluate workforce programs that were never randomized, with effect
estimates that vary credibly by place and time rather than a single
average.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03938 · **Library entry:** [2026-chetty-fogel-katz-digital-twins-workforce-training](../../../library/entries/2026-chetty-fogel-katz-digital-twins-workforce-training.md)
**Selected because:** High on causal inference and empirical methods (lens 5) — a usable, validated method for program evaluation without a new RCT for every program.

### 2. Prizes, Shared Hazard, and the Speed of Frontier AI (academic, Li Gan, 2026-11-03)

**Why it matters:** Decomposes how much of frontier-AI racing speed and
catastrophe risk comes from the competitive contest for leadership versus
labs' shared exposure to catastrophic hazard, engaging directly with the
real September 2026 White House Accord on Super Intelligence — in the
model, an audit-only accord with no speed limit does not slow the race,
while a common research-share rule would.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04266 · **Library entry:** [2026-gan-prizes-shared-hazard-frontier-ai-speed](../../../library/entries/2026-gan-prizes-shared-hazard-frontier-ai-speed.md)
**Selected because:** High on economic growth and competition/regulation (lenses 1, 4) — a quantified decomposition directly relevant to a live, named policy debate.

### 3. Agreements Have a Clock: Compute Growth and the Verification of AI Restraint (academic, Li Gan, 2026-11-03)

**Why it matters:** Formalizes why an AI-pause or restraint agreement has a
limited sustainable lifespan as compute keeps growing, and quantifies how
much verified safety-research reciprocity and cheaper monitoring extend
that lifespan — a concrete framework for the question any AI-pause proposal
must answer: not whether labs should slow down, but for how long such a
deal can actually hold.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04272 · **Library entry:** [2026-gan-agreements-have-a-clock-ai-restraint-verification](../../../library/entries/2026-gan-agreements-have-a-clock-ai-restraint-verification.md)
**Selected because:** High on competition, regulation and public policy (lens 4) — a formal, policy-usable account of AI-restraint agreement durability.

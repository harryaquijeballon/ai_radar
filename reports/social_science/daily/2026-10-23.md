## Today's developments

### 1. The Delegation Divide: An Open Index of AI-Agent Delegability across Occupations and Countries (academic, Cristian Espinal Maya & Santiago Jiménez Londoño, 2026-10-22)

**Why it matters:** A global, occupation-and-country-level index of how
much work can actually be delegated to AI agents today, extending the
mostly-US exposure literature (Eloundou, Felten) to 146 countries. The
income gradient in delegable work (22.5% low-income vs. 48.7% high-income)
and the finding that delegability tracks observed AI adoption evenly across
income groups are both directly citable for growth and development
analysis.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30752 · **Library entry:** [2026-espinalmaya-agent-delegability-index](../../../library/entries/2026-espinalmaya-agent-delegability-index.md)
**Selected because:** High on lens 1/2 — a preregistered, multiply-validated
delegability measure with a quantified cross-country income gradient.

### 2. Joules-to-GDP: How Much Economic Value Do AI Systems Produce per Unit of Energy? (academic, Zeinali, Saad-Falcon, Narayan, et al., 2026-10-22)

**Why it matters:** A directly measured, task-grounded metric for AI's
economic value net of energy cost — useful for productivity-measurement
debates that otherwise rely on benchmark accuracy or raw spend as a proxy
for AI's contribution, and relevant to AI-datacenter-and-growth accounting
questions.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30858 · **Library entry:** [2026-zeinali-joules-to-gdp](../../../library/entries/2026-zeinali-joules-to-gdp.md)
**Selected because:** High on lens 1 — a new, quantified productivity-per-
energy metric across 220 real tasks, 5 models and 4 accelerator types.

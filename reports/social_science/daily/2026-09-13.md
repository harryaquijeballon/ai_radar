### 1. But How Would AI Agents Run a Town's Economy? (academic, Sajal Regmi, Siddhartha Pudasaini, Chetan Phakami Pun, arXiv, 2026-09-10)

**Why it matters:** 100 memory-equipped LLM agents were placed in charge of a closed, money-conserving spatial economy (modeled on Pokhara Lakeside) and run for 91 validated simulations across 26 weeks. The result is not a frictionless toy: a 12x tourist-demand shock raised revenue only 4.62x, wages barely moved (1.03x, not significant), only 0.3% of menu items were repriced, and a randomized cash transfer left 96.7% of funds unspent (implied marginal propensity to consume of 3–4%). Wealth concentration loosened only slowly (correlation 0.964 at 2 weeks, 0.752 at 26 weeks). For researchers using or evaluating LLM-agent economic simulations, this is a concrete instance with real macro frictions reported *and* an explicit robustness caveat: swapping the underlying LLM materially changed outcomes, while removing agent memory did not.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11108 · **Library entry:** [2026-regmi-ai-agents-town-economy](../../../library/entries/2026-regmi-ai-agents-town-economy.md)
**Selected because:** a concrete, quantified generative-agent macro simulation with reported frictions and a stated model-sensitivity limitation — exactly what lens 6 asks for over an unvalidated demo.

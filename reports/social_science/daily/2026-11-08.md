## Today's developments

### 1. Does Frontier AI Strengthen Cyber Defense? Evidence from Project Glasswing (academic, Thomas Mosk, 2026-11-04)

**Why it matters:** A rare quantified, not modeled, answer to a live
AI-governance question: restricted early access to a frontier
vulnerability-finding model more than doubled access-firms' disclosures
(150% more high-impact ones) with no evidence attackers exploited the
extra flaws — but it also measurably widened the security gap between
access-firm countries and those without access. Directly usable in debates
over export controls and early-access programs for dual-use AI capability.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06629 · **Library entry:** [2026-mosk-frontier-ai-cyber-defense-glasswing](../../../library/entries/2026-mosk-frontier-ai-cyber-defense-glasswing.md)
**Selected because:** High on competition/regulation (lens 4) and political
science (lens 7, geopolitics of AI) — a measured natural-experiment result,
not commentary.

### 2. Dull, Dirty, Dangerous? Working Conditions and the Limits of Occupational AI Exposure (academic, Siyi Yu, 2026-11-04)

**Why it matters:** A direct quantified check on the routine claim that AI
will relieve workers of dull, dirty, or dangerous work: across 754 US
occupations, the association between AI exposure and bad working
conditions survives under one established exposure measure (AIOE) but
weakens to statistical non-significance under an LLM-based measure once
confounds are controlled. A usable caution against citing exposure scores
alone as evidence of AI's welfare effects on workers.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06888 · **Library entry:** [2026-yu-occupational-ai-exposure-working-conditions](../../../library/entries/2026-yu-occupational-ai-exposure-working-conditions.md)
**Selected because:** High on economic growth/productivity (lens 1) — a
measurable challenge to a widely repeated automation narrative.

### 3. Decision Models as Scalable Economic Surrogates that Capture Human Variation (academic, Maxime Saxena, Fabio Caccioli, R. Maria del Rio-Chanona, 2026-11-03)

**Why it matters:** LLMs used as simulated economic agents are known to
underrepresent human response variation — a live validity problem for any
research using them as survey or experiment stand-ins. This paper
benchmarks an alternative model class ("Decision Models") against 8 LLMs
across three economic settings and finds it reproduces human variation
better in all three, while running roughly 10x faster and 100x+ cheaper —
a concrete alternative for researchers who have been defaulting to LLMs.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05553 · **Library entry:** [2026-saxena-decision-models-economic-surrogates](../../../library/entries/2026-saxena-decision-models-economic-surrogates.md)
**Selected because:** High on AI applied to social-science research (lens
6) — a concrete, validated, benchmarked alternative, not a demo.

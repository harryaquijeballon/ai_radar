## Today's developments

### 1. AI Premium (academic — NBER Working Paper w35451, Nicola Borri, Aleh Tsyvinski, Yukun Liu, posted July 2026)

**Why it matters:** A usable dataset and method for anyone measuring AI's economic footprint: 380 trillion tokens of *realized* AI consumption (not adoption surveys) are used to build a firm-level "AI Beta" and an occupation-level AI-exposure measure. The reported asymmetries — a premium strongest for frontier-oriented use and absent in emerging markets like China, and higher market-implied exposure for interaction/communication occupations than for analytical/operations-control ones — are specific, citable claims directly usable in growth, labour-market, or development research.

**Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35451 · **Library entry:** [2026-borri-tsyvinski-liu-ai-premium](../../../library/entries/2026-borri-tsyvinski-liu-ai-premium.md)
**Selected because:** high on economic growth/productivity and digital-technologies lenses; NBER working paper page fetched and traced directly; a genuinely novel usage-based dataset and quantified method rather than a survey-based estimate.

### 2. Falling Behind Drives Unsafe Development in an Idealised AI Race Experiment (academic — arXiv:2607.26034, submitted 28 Jul 2026)

**Why it matters:** A controlled experimental challenge to AI-safety policy framings built around individual-lab risk tolerance: participants chose unsafe development paths not from risk preference but from trailing a competitor, regardless of their individual risk appetite. The stated policy implication — reduce competitive pressure and promote cooperation, not only individual risk management — is directly usable by anyone evaluating coordination-based versus compliance-based AI-safety policy.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26034 · **Library entry:** [2026-fernandez-domingos-ai-race-unsafe-development](../../../library/entries/2026-fernandez-domingos-ai-race-unsafe-development.md)
**Selected because:** high on competition, regulation and public policy; arXiv abstract fetched and traced directly; a quantified experimental result with a stated, actionable policy implication.

### 3. Generative Artificial Intelligence in Scientific Research: Individual Benefits, Collective Risks, and a Framework for Responsible Research with AI (academic — arXiv:2607.24879, submitted 27 Jul 2026)

**Why it matters:** Names the mechanisms (information asymmetry, negative externalities on shared knowledge, research-capacity depletion) by which individually rational AI use in research can still degrade the collective knowledge system, and proposes a four-principle governance vocabulary — disclosure, differentiation, narrative, proportionality — usable by anyone assessing AI-assisted research in review, funding, or replication contexts. Cross-domain with ai_engineering, where the same framework is medium relevance as institutional AI governance grounded in the EU AI Act and OECD guidance.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24879 · **Library entry:** [2026-castellacci-responsible-research-ai](../../../library/entries/2026-castellacci-responsible-research-ai.md)
**Selected because:** high on AI applied to social-science research; arXiv abstract fetched and traced directly; a structured, named-mechanism account plus a concrete governance framework rather than a general caution.

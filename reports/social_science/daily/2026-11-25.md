## Today's developments

### 1. Burning the Bridge: Agentification and Domestic Value Added (academic, Eduardo Levy Yeyati, 2026-11-24)

**Why it matters:** A quantified small-open-economy model showing that AI-agent adoption in local services can reduce domestic value added even when foreign agent costs stay flat, with a derived threshold (supplier bargaining share above ~0.15 for local services vs. ~0.66 for exporters) calibrated against Brazilian and Mexican national accounts — a specific, usable framework for which sectors of a developing or emerging economy are more exposed to AI-driven outsourcing dependence.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31518 · **Library entry:** [2026-levyyeyati-agentification-domestic-value-added](../../../library/entries/2026-levyyeyati-agentification-domestic-value-added.md)
**Selected because:** a quantified growth/development model with a concrete, country-calibrated threshold — lens 1 (economic growth and productivity) and lens 2 (economic development).

### 2. Training decision models for simulating human respondents (academic, Davood Wadi, Yu Ma, 2026-11-25)

**Why it matters:** Direct, quantified evidence on the credibility frontier of using LLMs as simulated survey respondents: off-the-shelf models skew treatment-effect estimates and neither larger proprietary models nor more reasoning effort fixes this, while purpose-trained decision models reduce treatment-effect error by 22%–89% across four held-out studies the model was never trained on.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31613 · **Library entry:** [2026-wadi-ma-decision-models-simulated-respondents](../../../library/entries/2026-wadi-ma-decision-models-simulated-respondents.md)
**Selected because:** a quantified fix for a known validity problem in AI-simulated research subjects — lens 6 (AI applied to social-science research, credibility and validation).

## Today's developments

### 1. When AI Gets Cheaper, Do Prices Follow? Information Delays and Algorithmic Pricing (academic, Krish Tadigotla, 2026-10-20)

**Why it matters:** A quantified simulation finding that AI-driven cost
reductions pass through to consumers at only 44.0-55.6% even under the
fastest information/repricing conditions, with consumer surplus below the
competitive benchmark in every tested condition — a specific, citable
figure for competition-policy arguments about whether AI cost savings
reach consumers or are retained as margin under algorithmic pricing.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31693 · **Library entry:** [2026-tadigotla-ai-pricing-passthrough](../../../library/entries/2026-tadigotla-ai-pricing-passthrough.md)
**Selected because:** High on competition, regulation and public policy (lens 4) — a 960-run factorial simulation with a specific, citable pass-through range rather than a qualitative collusion worry.

### 2. AI-Augmented Research: Free to Write, Costly to Read (academic, Wolfgang Kuhle, 2026-10-23)

**Why it matters:** A formal mechanism for why cheap AI-assisted writing
can collapse the incentive to verify research — reader time scarcity
means impact depends increasingly on author reputation alone — with a
specific, named risk (correlated AI-summarization errors) for anyone
relying on AI to navigate the resulting volume. Sharpens the question of
whether research built on model outputs can be known to be right.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32355 · **Library entry:** [2026-kuhle-write-cheap-read-costly-ai-research](../../../library/entries/2026-kuhle-write-cheap-read-costly-ai-research.md)
**Selected because:** High on philosophy of AI and trust (lens 8) — a mechanism-level model of the epistemology-of-AI-generated-knowledge question, not a general restatement of it.

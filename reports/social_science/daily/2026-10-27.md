## Today's developments

### 1. Efficiency vs Capability in the Intelligence Explosion (academic, Karthik Tadepalli, 2026-10-24)

**Why it matters:** Gives economists a quantified, falsifiable framework —
rather than a qualitative argument — for assessing "intelligence explosion"
claims about AI's effect on the pace of AI research itself, and identifies
efficiency-progress measurement as the key open empirical question.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33447 · **Library entry:** [2026-tadepalli-intelligence-explosion-efficiency-capability](../../../library/entries/2026-tadepalli-intelligence-explosion-efficiency-capability.md)
**Selected because:** Quantifies, via a calibrated task-automation model and Monte Carlo simulation (49% probability of a software-driven explosion), how AI capability and efficiency gains jointly determine growth-relevant acceleration claims (lens 1).

### 2. The Capability Transfer: Cohort Reversal, Codified Expertise, and the Adaptability Tax of AI Transformation (academic, Ina Catrinescu, 2026-10-22)

**Why it matters:** A concrete, falsifiable model of how AI automation of codified junior work degrades the pipeline that produces senior expertise, with three specific indicators an organization or researcher could track.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33102 · **Library entry:** [2026-catrinescu-capability-transfer-expertise-pipeline](../../../library/entries/2026-catrinescu-capability-transfer-expertise-pipeline.md)
**Selected because:** Usable firm-level framework with quantified baseline scenarios (time-to-expert rising from ~5 to ~9 years) and proposed measurement indicators for AI's effect on human-capital formation (lens 1).

### 3. Prospect Theory in Large Language Models: A Meta-Analysis of Loss Aversion, Diminishing Sensitivity, and Probability Weighting (academic, Adi Tarabeih, 2026-10-22)

**Why it matters:** A quantified map of where LLM "economic behaviour" does and doesn't match human behaviour, including a method-dependence finding directly relevant to anyone using LLMs as simulated subjects in behavioural/experimental economics.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33103 · **Library entry:** [2026-tarabeih-prospect-theory-llm-meta-analysis](../../../library/entries/2026-tarabeih-prospect-theory-llm-meta-analysis.md)
**Selected because:** Pooled meta-analysis (102 estimates, 11 studies, 32 models) giving a credible, quantified warning about specific validity failure modes of LLMs as simulated economic agents (lens 6).

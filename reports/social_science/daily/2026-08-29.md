## Today's developments

### 1. Sophistication in GenAI Use: Field Evidence from a Large Firm (academic, Nicholas J. Hallman, Zachary T. Kowaleski, Anu Puvvada, Jaime J. Schmidt, 2026-08-27)

**Why it matters:** A rare within-firm, prompt-level measure of how well employees use genAI, not just whether they use it — drawn from 713,564 prompts across nearly 4,000 employees over eight months. Senior employees and strategy/innovation-focused functions show more sophisticated use, and sophistication did not respond to formal AI training, a concrete data point against the "just add training" assumption common in AI-adoption policy discussions.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27364 · **Library entry:** [2026-hallman-genai-sophistication-firm](../../../library/entries/2026-hallman-genai-sophistication-firm.md)
**Selected because:** high on economic growth and productivity (lens 1) — quantified, firm-level, actionable for researchers and managers studying AI adoption.

### 2. Liberal Institutions Are Dead / Long Live Liberal Institutions (commentary, Harry Law — Cosmos Institute, 2026-08-28)

**Why it matters:** Gives a specific causal mechanism — the collapse of the "costly effort" that historically rationed institutional participation — for why AI agents strain courts, regulators, and ombuds bodies, anchored by a named regulator's own workload projection (Australia's Fair Work Commission, +70% in three years). Directly useful for readers thinking about AI's effects on political institutions and governance capacity.

**Source:** https://blog.cosmos-institute.org/p/liberal-institutions-are-dead · **Library entry:** [2026-law-liberal-institutions-ai-agents](../../../library/entries/2026-law-liberal-institutions-ai-agents.md)
**Selected because:** high on political science (lens 7) and philosophy of AI and trust (lens 8) — this profile's deliberate counterweight lenses, with a concrete institutional mechanism rather than general commentary.

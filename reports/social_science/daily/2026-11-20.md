## Today's developments

### 1. Structural economic change under the digital transformation (academic, Marco Dueñas, Antoine Mandel — arXiv preprint, 2026-11-19)

**Why it matters:** Gives economists modelling AI/digital effects on growth a reusable distinction — whether digital inputs act as ordinary intermediate goods or are combined directly with primary factors — that changes predicted output, value-added, and labour-reallocation outcomes; a mechanism for why headline GDP gains from digital-driven productivity growth can overstate gains to workers.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26338 · **Library entry:** [2026-duenas-mandel-structural-change-digital-transformation](../../../library/entries/2026-duenas-mandel-structural-change-digital-transformation.md)
**Selected because:** High on lens 1 (economic growth and productivity) and lens 3 (digital technologies as economic inputs) — a quantified agent-based modelling framework, not a qualitative claim.

### 2. The American Medical Directory Panel: Comparing AI-Assisted and LLM Transcription with Manual Data Entry at Scale (academic, Christian M. Dahl, Sophie Li, Rasmus Emil Nyholm, Maja U. Pedersen, Anthony Wray — arXiv preprint, 2026-11-18)

**Why it matters:** Gives social-science researchers a validated template for building historical or large-scale datasets with AI assistance — a rule-based-parser-plus-commercial-OCR pipeline matched manual double-entry transcription at ~97-98% agreement, at roughly 1/500th the manual cost, with disagreements reported rather than hidden behind an aggregate accuracy number.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25218 · **Library entry:** [2026-dahl-american-medical-directory-panel-transcription](../../../library/entries/2026-dahl-american-medical-directory-panel-transcription.md)
**Selected because:** High on lens 6 (AI applied to social-science research) — a concrete, validated application, not a demo.

### 3. AI Might Be Flooding State Court Houses, but Proving It Is Another Matter (academic, Javokhir Arifov, Alexander Spangher, Jonathan Hennessy, Diya Karan, Emaan Hariri, Daniel E. Ho — Stanford HAI/RegLab, 2026-11-19)

**Why it matters:** Gives social scientists tracking AI's institutional and governance effects a rare multi-jurisdiction, time-trending measurement of AI-generated text reaching US court filings (rising from near zero in 2022 to over a third of examined Santa Clara complaints in 2026), alongside a concrete governance lever — better AI-assistance disclosure policy — rather than only a warning.

**Source:** https://hai.stanford.edu/news/ai-might-be-flooding-state-court-houses-but-proving-it-is-another-matter · **Library entry:** [2026-arifov-spangher-ho-ai-text-state-courts](../../../library/entries/2026-arifov-spangher-ho-ai-text-state-courts.md)

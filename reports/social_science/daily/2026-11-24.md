## Today's developments

### 1. AI-Assisted Programming and Labor Demand (academic, Lindsey Raymond, Eleanor Wiske Dillon, 2026-11-23)

**Why it matters:** A causally identified (procurement-cycle natural experiment, not merely correlational) estimate across 5,414 firms — about 38% of the U.S. programming workforce — finding GitHub Copilot adoption cut quarterly technical hiring by 29% a year after adoption, concentrated in junior roles, with no detectable effect on separations or headcount. A specific, falsifiable magnitude for the junior-labor-market-displacement debate.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31083 · **Library entry:** [2026-raymond-dillon-ai-assisted-programming-labor-demand](../../../library/entries/2026-raymond-dillon-ai-assisted-programming-labor-demand.md)
**Selected because:** a causally identified, large-sample labour-demand estimate for an AI coding tool — lens 1 (economic growth and productivity) and lens 4 (regulation/labour-market evidence).

### 2. Working with AI (primary, Nathan Wilmers, Maxim Massenkoff, Peter McCrory — The Anthropic Institute, 2026-11-23)

**Why it matters:** Interview-based (not survey-elicited) fieldwork across 58 US enterprise-AI-adopter firms finds domestic layoffs attributable to AI automation are rare, while reduced hiring and cuts to offshore contractors are the more common channel — a concrete, qualitative-but-detailed data point for the displacement-vs-reorganization debate, with the authors flagging a possible early-warning signal for the international business-services industry.

**Source:** https://www.anthropic.com/research/working-with-ai · **Library entry:** [2026-wilmers-working-with-ai-enterprise-adoption](../../../library/entries/2026-wilmers-working-with-ai-enterprise-adoption.md)
**Selected because:** a rare interview-based (not survey) look at how AI adoption reshapes hiring and contracting across 58 firms — lens 1 (growth/productivity) and lens 4 (labour-market evidence).

## Today's developments

### 1. Data-Center Growth Impacts Household Electricity Costs More Through Wholesale Prices Than Through Grid Reinforcement (academic, Lingdong Zhou et al. — arXiv preprint, 2026-11-11)

**Why it matters:** A quantified, scenario-based answer to a policy question regulators are actively litigating — who pays for the electricity data centers draw. About 68% of the residential cost increase from AI-driven data-center growth comes from higher wholesale prices rather than transmission investment, and the specific levers tested (generation siting, cost-sharing-base rules) shift the household impact by roughly a factor of two.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16095 · **Library entry:** [2026-zhou-datacenter-electricity-wholesale-prices](../../../library/entries/2026-zhou-datacenter-electricity-wholesale-prices.md)
**Selected because:** High on lens 3 (digital/AI infrastructure as an economic input) and lens 4 (regulation), with named, numbered cost-allocation levers a regulator could act on.

### 2. The Rebundling of Freelance Work in the AI Age (academic, Caroline Chin et al. — arXiv preprint, 2026-11-10)

**Why it matters:** A rare direct comparison of real AI-usage data (ChatGPT) against a real labour marketplace (Upwork), rather than survey self-report or task-exposure proxies. It gives a falsifiable, testable pattern — task share shifting with ChatGPT-activity prevalence, and AI-related work rebundling into larger, higher-value projects — that a labour economist could test against other platforms or national data.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14708 · **Library entry:** [2026-chin-rebundling-freelance-ai-age](../../../library/entries/2026-chin-rebundling-freelance-ai-age.md)
**Selected because:** High on lens 1 (economic growth and productivity): quantified AI-and-labour evidence from marketplace data, not self-report.

### 3. Inference Performance Index: Changing the Way We Measure Inference Capacity (academic, Jason Li — Epoch AI, 2026-11-09)

**Why it matters:** A rigorously specified, externally validated metric (checked against independent SemiAnalysis measurements, ~12% average error) for how much inference capacity a chip actually delivers — directly usable as an economic input measure for anyone analysing AI infrastructure investment or capacity.

**Source:** https://epoch.ai/publications/inference-performance-index · **Library entry:** [2026-li-epoch-inference-performance-index](../../../library/entries/2026-li-epoch-inference-performance-index.md)
**Selected because:** High on lens 3 (AI/digital infrastructure as an economic input), with a stated, checkable error bound rather than being purely self-reported modelling.

## Today's developments

### 1. Shopping by Algorithm: How Agentic AI Deploys Human Heuristics as a Surrogate Consumer (academic, Davood Wadi & Yu Ma — arXiv preprint, 2026-09-23)

**Why it matters:** A controlled test of eight LLMs across three providers as shopping agents shows classic manipulative pricing tactics barely work when information is free, but a realistic combination of paid information and vague user instructions reopens the same vulnerability humans have — with a concrete fix (specific instructions) available to the person delegating the purchase. Directly usable evidence for platform-design and consumer-protection questions raised by delegated AI purchasing.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28372 · **Library entry:** [2026-wadi-ma-shopping-by-algorithm-price-cues](../../../library/entries/2026-wadi-ma-shopping-by-algorithm-price-cues.md)
**Selected because:** High on lens 4 (competition, regulation and public policy) — a quantified, conditional answer on manipulation resistance, not speculation.

### 2. Mining Meaning: Measurement Error in AI-Assisted Literature Reviews (academic, Jeffrey D. Michler, Kieran Douglas & Anna Josephson — arXiv preprint, 2026-09-23)

**Why it matters:** Tests LLM-assisted literature review as a measurement-error problem rather than an accuracy question, finding that errors hit paper-level conclusions far harder than aggregate literature-level claims. A template for evaluating any AI-assisted research workflow before trusting its output, not just this one.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27686 · **Library entry:** [2026-michler-mining-meaning-ai-literature-review-error](../../../library/entries/2026-michler-mining-meaning-ai-literature-review-error.md)
**Selected because:** High on lens 6 (AI applied to social-science research) — a concrete, evaluable test of a specific AI-assisted-research workflow.

### 3. When Trust Attracts Fraud: AI and Trust Arbitrage (academic, Xieyu Yin & Fenghua Wen — Economics Letters, 2026-09-23)

**Why it matters:** A formal model shows AI-driven verification-cost reductions push fraud toward higher-quality, slower-to-verify markets first, and that this is self-correcting once the resulting fraud inflow triggers verification. Gives fraud-and-verification policy debates a specific, falsifiable target (accelerate verification in high-quality markets) rather than a generic "AI enables fraud" warning.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27404 · **Library entry:** [2026-yin-wen-trust-arbitrage-ai-fraud](../../../library/entries/2026-yin-wen-trust-arbitrage-ai-fraud.md)
**Selected because:** High on lens 4 — a named, testable mechanism published in a peer-reviewed venue.

### 1. Your AI Adoption Lift Is a Selection Effect (commentary, William Gieng, Towards Data Science, 2026-09-13)

**Why it matters:** Comparisons of AI-feature adopters against non-adopters overstate the feature's effect because adoption itself signals readiness, not random assignment — on synthetic data the naive adopter gap (+15.4pp) is nearly four times the simulated true effect (+4pp). The fix demonstrated here is a fuzzy regression-discontinuity design keyed to an arbitrary, customer-unchosen rollout eligibility threshold (e.g., a seat-count minimum), with runnable code and diagnostics. For anyone evaluating claims that an AI feature "lifted" some outcome — including several already in this library — this is a directly applicable identification strategy when adoption wasn't randomized.

**Selected because:** a concrete, reusable causal-inference pattern for a recurring measurement problem in AI-adoption claims — lens 5, directly usable.

## Today's developments

### 1. Grokipedia Stretches the US Political Spectrum (academic, Philipp Koch, Alexander Posztos, 2026-11-24)

**Why it matters:** A large-sample (1,614 politicians) comparison of Grokipedia's and Wikipedia's biography framing finds that Grokipedia doesn't simply favor one party — it systematically stretches the political spectrum, with the perceived conservatism of a biography's framing rising roughly twice as steeply with the subject's own ideology on Grokipedia as on Wikipedia. A concrete, quantified case of AI-platform editorial choices measurably amplifying political polarization.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32420 · **Library entry:** [2026-koch-posztos-grokipedia-political-spectrum](../../../library/entries/2026-koch-posztos-grokipedia-political-spectrum.md)
**Selected because:** quantified empirical result on AI's effects on political framing — lens 7 (political science, platform power).

### 2. AI, Automation, and Fiscal Pressure (academic, Spencer Bastani, Georg Graetz, Bernat Pla, Oskar N. Skans, 2026-11-25)

**Why it matters:** A calibrated two-sector general-equilibrium model shows automation can widen fiscal deficits even amid AI-driven growth: in the EU-27 benchmark, the deficit rises by almost one percentage point of value added despite the economy growing about 12% from automation, because automation shifts income from heavily-taxed labor toward lightly-taxed capital. A concrete counterweight to assuming AI growth automatically eases public-finance pressure.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34234 · **Library entry:** [2026-bastani-graetz-automation-fiscal-pressure](../../../library/entries/2026-bastani-graetz-automation-fiscal-pressure.md)
**Selected because:** a usable calibrated model for growth-vs-fiscal-sustainability analysis — lens 1 (growth) and lens 4 (policy evaluation).

### 3. Will AI Replace Juniors? How Task Complexity Reduces Automation Risk (academic, Vincent Rost, Fabian Stephany, 2026-11-25)

**Why it matters:** A pre-registered survey experiment across 1,435 German firms finds task complexity — not just seniority — protects junior workers from AI-driven hiring substitution: complex tasks reduced managers' willingness to substitute AI for an entry-level hire by 0.54 points on a five-point scale. Managers' baseline expectation, though, is still that AI will cut entry-level hiring more than experienced hiring.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34099 · **Library entry:** [2026-rost-stephany-ai-replace-juniors](../../../library/entries/2026-rost-stephany-ai-replace-juniors.md)
**Selected because:** quantified causal-experiment evidence on AI and labor-market structure by task type — lens 1 (growth and productivity).

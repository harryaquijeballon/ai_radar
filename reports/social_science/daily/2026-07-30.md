### 1. The Human Utility Factor: A Computable Welfare Metric That Reframes AI Governance as a Constrained Optimisation Problem (academic — arXiv:2607.26068, Sivasathivel Kandasamy, cross-listed to econ.GN 30 Jul 2026)

**Why it matters:** A usable analytical object for anyone evaluating AI-and-labour-market policy: the paper reports a closed-form optimal automation level and a minimum redistribution threshold, derived from a three-dimension welfare model (Agency, Wellbeing, Economic Stability) and tested via multi-agent reinforcement learning across U.S., Canadian, and Nordic policy scenarios. Its central warning — that welfare metrics without an explicit redistribution constraint can rationalise high-automation, low-welfare outcomes — is a specific, actionable caution for anyone designing or citing AI-policy welfare metrics.

### 2. One Run Is Not an Idea: The Implementation Lottery in Automated Research (academic — arXiv:2607.26587, Jingjie Ning et al., submitted 29 Jul 2026)

**Why it matters:** A quantified, credible warning about a method failure mode directly applicable to automated and agentic research pipelines — including the agentic economic-research workflows this radar already tracks: single experimental runs are unreliable evidence for idea-level conclusions, with the winning approach flipping in roughly 25.6%–43.6% of decisions across alternative implementations of the same idea. Cross-domain with ai_engineering, where it is equally high relevance as an evaluation/validation lesson for agentic research systems generally.
